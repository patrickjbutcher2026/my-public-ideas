## 🏛️ Case Study: The “Mackinac Autonomy” Home
**Location:** Mackinac Island, MI (Latitude 45.8° N)  
**Subject:** 1,800 sq ft, 3‑bedroom single‑story residence  
**Context:** No cars, high fuel transport costs, cold winters, strong summer sun.

This case study applies the Solar Thermal Roadmap to a real northern‑climate home with Victorian‑plus constraints.

---

### Phase 1 — Sanitation & Survival (Thermosiphon Utility)
**Goal:** 100% domestic hot water without propane or electric heating.

**Setup**
- Three flat‑plate collectors (~96 sq ft total)  
- South‑facing roof  
- 120‑gallon insulated attic tank (above collectors)  

**Performance**
- **Summer:** ~150 gallons/day at 60 °C  
- **Winter:** Preheats 4 °C well water to ~30 °C  

**Advantages**
- Zero moving parts  
- Repairable with copper pipe and solder  
- Freeze‑resistant when designed correctly  

Learn more: [Explore thermosiphon systems](ca://s?q=Explore_thermosiphon_systems)

### Phase 2 — Food & Climate (Solar Porch + IcyBall)
**Goal:** Off‑grid refrigeration and wood‑free cooking.

**Setup**
- 20 sq ft solar box oven  
- Medium parabolic cooker  
- 1,000 lbs of brick/stone thermal mass in a south‑facing mudroom  

**Performance**
- **Cooking:** 175 °C (350°F) by noon in Feb/March on clear days  
- **Cooling:** IcyBall fridge — 2 hours of solar concentration → 24 hours of refrigeration  

**Advantages**
- Preserves garden harvests  
- Reduces propane dependence  
- Works even in cold climates  

Learn more: [Explore passive thermal architecture](ca://s?q=Explore_passive_thermal_architecture)

### Phase 2.5 — Bootstrap Engines (The “Steam Shed”)
**Goal:** 0.5–1 horsepower of early mechanical power.

**Setup**
- 200 sq ft linear parabolic trough array  
- Low‑pressure boiler  
- Small oscillating or atmospheric engine  

**Performance**
- Drives a wood‑splitter, pump, or treadle‑lathe  
- Uses ~400 sq ft of yard space  

**Advantages**
- Enables fabrication of better collectors  
- Provides the first mechanical power on the property  

Learn more: [Explore zero‑precision engines](ca://s?q=Explore_zero_precision_engines)

---

### Phase 3 — Mechanical Workshop (Precision Emergence)
**Goal:** 5 horsepower of reliable mechanical work.

**Setup**
- Improved trough array  
- Pressurized steam accumulator (“thermal battery”)  
- Small workshop steam engine  

**Performance**
- Powers lathes, saws, grinders, pumps  
- Manufactures replacement parts locally  

**Advantages**
- Critical for island life where hardware stores are inaccessible  
- Enables self‑repair and toolmaking  

---

### Phase 4 — Industrial Micro‑Grid (Winter Resilience)
**Goal:** Baseload electricity and space heating through dark months.

**Setup**
- Mini solar tower with ~20 heliostats  
- Buried 15' × 15' rock‑bed heat store  
- Small steam turbine + NiFe battery bank  

**Performance**
- Rock bed keeps slab at ~18 °C (65°F) into November  
- 2–3 kWh/day of electricity  

**Advantages**
- True energy independence  
- Works even with low winter sun

## 📊 Solar Thermal vs. Solar PV (Mackinac Context)

| Feature | Solar PV | Solar Thermal |
|--------|----------|----------------|
| Efficiency | ~20% | ~75% |
| Maintenance | Electronics, inverters | Mechanical, wrench‑fixable |
| Winter Performance | Poor | Strong (high‑temp collectors) |
| End‑of‑Life | E‑waste | Recyclable (steel, copper, glass) |
| Island Factor | Inverter failure = outage | Fully repairable locally |

---

## 💡 Conclusion
For this home, **Phase 1 and 2** cover ~80% of energy needs using only ~100 sq ft of roof space.  
Phase 4 requires ~2,000 sq ft of yard — but provides unmatched resilience on an island dependent on a single underwater cable.
