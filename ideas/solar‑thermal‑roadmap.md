# Village‑Scale Solar Thermal Roadmap
*A high‑level progression from low‑tech heat to industrial‑level mechanical power.*

---

## 🌞 Phase 1 — Sanitation & Survival
**Core technologies:** flat‑plate collectors, [thermosiphon](ca://s?q=Explore_thermosiphon_systems)  
**Temperature range:** 40–70 °C  
**Goal:** Replace firewood for hygiene and basic domestic heat.

**Outputs**
- Hot water for washing, laundry, sanitation  
- Water pasteurization (65 °C for 6 minutes)  
- Feedwater preheating for future steam systems  

**Why it matters**  
This is the lowest‑tech, highest‑impact solar application. It reduces fuel use, improves health, and requires no precision machining.

---

## 🔥 Phase 2 — Food & Climate
**Core technologies:** solar box ovens, parabolic cookers, [passive thermal architecture](ca://s?q=Explore_passive_thermal_architecture)  
**Temperature range:** 100–200 °C  
**Goal:** Food preservation, cooking, and basic climate control.

**Outputs**
- Crop dehydration  
- Grain roasting  
- Solar cooking  
- Ammonia‑absorption refrigeration (“IcyBall”)  

**Why it matters**  
This phase stabilizes food supply and reduces dependence on biomass fuels.

---

## ⚙️ Phase 2.5 — Bootstrap Engines
**Core technologies:** atmospheric engines, oscillating engines, crude hot‑air engines, [solar piston engines](ca://s?q=Explore_zero_precision_engines)  
**Temperature range:** low‑pressure steam or hot air  
**Goal:** Create the *first mechanical power* using engines that require minimal precision.

**Outputs**
- 0.1–1 horsepower  
- Water pumping  
- Grain milling  
- Early machine‑tool assistance  
- Charging mechanical storage (flywheels, elevated water)  

**Why it matters**  
This is the “tools to make the tools” phase. It bridges the precision gap and enables Phase 3.

---

## 🛠️ Phase 3 — The Mechanical Dawn
**Core technologies:** parabolic troughs, Fresnel reflectors, small steam engines  
**Temperature range:** 250–400 °C  
**Goal:** Convert heat into reliable mechanical work.

**Outputs**
- Lathes, drill presses, saws  
- Pumps, mills, workshop tools  
- Manufacturing of better solar collectors  
- Irrigation and workshop mechanization  

**Why it matters**  
This is where a village becomes a workshop — and a workshop becomes an industry.

---

## 🏭 Phase 4 — Industrial Micro‑Grid
**Core technologies:** solar tower, heliostat field, molten‑salt storage  
**Temperature range:** 500 °C+  
**Goal:** Baseload power and industrial‑level heat.

**Outputs**
- 24/7 electricity via steam turbine  
- Forge‑level heat for metalworking  
- Glass production  
- High‑temperature chemical processes  

**Why it matters**  
This is the threshold where a community achieves true industrial autonomy.

---

## 🔋 Thermal Storage Overview
A village climbs the storage ladder as it climbs the thermal ladder.

### Phase 1 — Hot Water Tanks
Low‑temperature sensible heat for sanitation and cooking.

### Phase 2 — Rock/Brick Beds
Medium‑temperature storage for ovens, dryers, and process heat.

### Phase 2.5 — Mechanical Storage
Flywheels, elevated water, compressed air.

### Phase 3 — Thermal Oils
High‑temperature sensible heat for steam generation.

### Phase 4 — Molten Salts + High‑Temp Rock Beds
Overnight electricity and industrial heat.

---

## 📈 Industrial Ladder
**Heat → Food → Cooling → Crude Engines → Precision Engines → Electricity → Industry**

This ladder is the backbone of [low‑tech infrastructure philosophy](ca://s?q=Create_low_tech_philosophy_file).
