# Mechanical Energy Shed
*A line‑shaft‑based, lever‑controlled energy routing system for post‑electronic infrastructure.*

The Energy Shed is a universal interface between **prime movers** (steam, wind, water, Stirling) and **storage modes** (thermal, electrical, mechanical, chemical).  
It replaces electronic control systems with **human‑operated clutches, belts, gears, and valves**.

---

## ⚙️ Prime Movers
Any mechanical source can feed the main line shaft:

- Steam engine (Phase 2.5–4)
- Stirling engine (crude or refined)
- Windmill shaft (Windhouse integration)
- Waterwheel or turbine
- Solar piston‑in‑focus engine

Each prime mover connects through a **clutch** so it can be engaged or disengaged safely.

## 🧵 Main Line Shaft (Mechanical Bus)
A rotating steel shaft running the length of the shed.

**Functions**
- Carries mechanical power to all subsystems  
- Allows selective engagement of loads  
- Provides a “mechanical grid” before electricity exists  

**Components**
- Bearings  
- Pulleys  
- Belts  
- Clutches  
- Flywheel (optional smoothing)

## 🔀 Branch Loads (Energy Destinations)
Each subsystem connects to the line shaft through a clutch, belt shifter, or gear selector.

### 1. Thermal Storage
**Shaft → pump → hot oil/water → rock bed or tank**

Used for:
- Rock‑bed heat storage  
- Hot‑water tanks  
- Thermal oil loops  
- Molten‑salt charging (advanced)

### 2. Electrical Storage
**Shaft → generator → DC bus → batteries**

- Small PM generator  
- NiFe or lead‑acid batteries  
- Faraday‑caged “electric nook”

### 3. Compressed Air
**Shaft → piston or rotary compressor → pressure tank**

Used for:
- Tools  
- Pumps  
- Short‑term energy storage  

### 4. Chemical Storage (Advanced)
**Shaft → generator → DC → electrolyzer**

Produces:
- Hydrogen  
- Oxygen  

Useful for:
- Metalworking  
- Fuel cells (optional)  
- Chemical heat storage

## 🎚️ Control Interface (The “Mechanical Knob”)
The Energy Shed replaces digital control systems with **physical levers**.

A typical control wall might include:

- **Lever 1:** Engage generator (charge batteries)  
- **Lever 2:** Engage air compressor  
- **Lever 3:** Pump hot water to storage  
- **Lever 4:** Run electrolyzer  
- **Lever 5:** Divert shaft to workshop tools  

Each lever:
- Engages a clutch  
- Moves a belt from idler to driven pulley  
- Opens a valve  
- Selects a gear  

This creates a **human‑centered, legible, fail‑safe control system**.

## 🧱 Layout Concept (12×20 ft Shed)
A typical Energy Shed layout:

**North Wall:**  
- Prime mover corner (steam/Stirling/wind shaft entry)  
- Flywheel + main clutch  

**Center:**  
- Line shaft running the full length  
- Overhead belts to branch loads  

**South Wall:**  
- Control levers  
- Valve manifold  
- Belt shifter handles  

**East Side:**  
- Thermal storage pumps  
- Hot‑oil manifold  
- Rock‑bed inlet/outlet  

**West Side:**  
- Generator + battery nook (Faraday cage)  
- Air compressor + tanks  
- Electrolyzer (optional)  

This layout keeps heat, electricity, and mechanical systems **physically separated** for safety and clarity.

## 🧭 Why the Energy Shed Matters
- No semiconductors  
- No fragile electronics  
- No software  
- No inverters  
- Fully repairable with hand tools  
- Legible to anyone  
- Safe failure modes  
- Works with any prime mover  

It is the **mechanical equivalent of a microgrid**, designed for resilience, autonomy, and human agency.
