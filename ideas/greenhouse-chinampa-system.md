# Greenhouse Chinampa System
*A low‑maintenance, high‑fertility, household‑scale food production pattern.*

## Purpose
The greenhouse chinampa system provides a compact, biologically active growing environment that delivers steady yields without overproduction or daily labor. It is designed for a single household seeking long‑term food resilience with minimal inputs and predictable upkeep.

The goal is to replace many small, high‑maintenance containers with one integrated, water‑supported growing bed that maintains fertility through continuous nutrient cycling.

## System Overview
The system consists of three core elements:

- a shallow **water basin**  
- a raised **soil island**  
- optional **vertical growing layers**  

The water stabilizes temperature, recycles nutrients, and supports aquatic life.  
The soil island supports vegetables, herbs, and trellised crops.  
The greenhouse protects the system from weather extremes and extends the growing season.

Optional fish such as tilapia, carp, or goldfish can be added to increase nutrient availability and provide supplemental protein.

## Key Advantages
- **Stable fertility** through continuous cycling of nutrients from water, fish waste, and organic matter.  
- **Low water use** due to upward wicking and minimal evaporation.  
- **Reduced daily labor** since watering, fertilizing, and repotting are minimized.  
- **High space efficiency** when combined with trellises or upper growing layers.  
- **Temperature moderation** from the water mass, stabilizing root zones and greenhouse climate.  
- **Long system lifespan** with minimal soil replacement.

## Limitations and Considerations
- **Higher initial construction effort** than pots or simple raised beds.  
- **Less flexibility** because the basin and island are fixed structures.  
- **Humidity management** may require ventilation in smaller greenhouses.  
- **Seasonal tasks** such as dredging silt or pruning roots require moderate labor.  
- **Warm‑water fish** require consistently warm water if included.

## Maintenance Expectations
Routine tasks:
- topping up water  
- managing plant growth  
- occasional cleaning of channels  

Seasonal tasks:
- adding organic matter  
- dredging nutrient‑rich silt from the basin  
- checking structural components  

Compared to container gardening, the system eliminates repetitive chores and reduces the need for soil replacement or constant fertilization.

## Suitability for Household‑Scale Production
A single chinampa island sized around **4×8 to 4×12 feet** can supply a steady stream of vegetables for a family without producing excess.  
Crop selection and planting density can be tuned to match consumption.

The system supports:
- greens  
- herbs  
- tomatoes  
- peppers  
- cucumbers  
- other common household crops  

Fish can be included for additional protein and nutrient cycling.

## Summary
A greenhouse chinampa system offers a practical balance of efficiency, sustainability, and manageable upkeep for household‑scale food production. It reduces ongoing labor, stabilizes soil fertility, and uses space effectively while avoiding the overproduction associated with larger agricultural systems.

The main tradeoff is the initial construction effort, which is offset by long‑term reductions in maintenance and input costs.
