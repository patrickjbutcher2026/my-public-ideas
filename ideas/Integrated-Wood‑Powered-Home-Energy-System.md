# Integrated Wood‑Powered Multi‑Output Energy Hub

## 🧩 Overview
A resilient, modular, wood‑powered micro‑utility that converts a single fire into multiple usable energy forms:

- Space heat  
- Cooking heat  
- Domestic hot water  
- Wood gas (syngas)  
- Mechanical power  
- Compressed air  
- Electrical energy  
- Short‑term mechanical storage (flywheel)  
- Long‑term thermal storage  

The system is housed in a dedicated fire‑resistant outbuilding (“energy shed”) for safety, maintenance, and modular expansion.

## 🔧 Requirements
- High‑efficiency wood stove/boiler  
- Thermal storage tank (300–800 L)  
- Piping, valves, heat exchangers  
- Wood gasifier module  
- Stirling or steam engine  
- Mechanical selector (belts, pulleys, clutches)  
- Air compressor + tanks  
- Generator head  
- Battery bank + inverter  
- Fire‑resistant shed  
- CO detectors, pressure relief valves  
- Venting and draft control  

## 💰 Resource Intensity Summary
- **Low:** Basic plumbing, CO detectors, simple shed  
- **Medium:** Thermal tank, gasifier, small Stirling, compressor  
- **High:** Steam engine, large insulated tank, fire‑resistant shed, battery bank  

## 🛡️ Safety Summary
- Sealed, vented gasifier  
- CO detection in shed + home  
- Pressure relief valves on all tanks  
- Fireproof construction  
- Proper chimney draft  
- Regular inspection of piping and tanks  

## 🌱 Benefits
- Local renewable fuel  
- Multi‑output energy from one fire  
- Reduces reliance on grid or fossil fuels  
- EMP‑proof and electronics‑optional  
- Integrates with solar  
- Highly modular and scalable  

## 🪜 High‑Level Steps
1. Build fire‑resistant energy shed  
2. Install stove/boiler + flue  
3. Add thermal battery + plumbing  
4. Add Stirling/steam engine  
5. Add mechanical selector  
6. Add compressor + tanks  
7. Add generator + battery bank  
8. Integrate solar  
9. Add distribution lines to home  

## ⭐ Why This Matters
This system transforms a simple wood fire into a **multi‑output energy hub** capable of powering a modern home with minimal external inputs — ideal for off‑grid living, resilience, and long‑term sustainability.

# **Full Technical Proposal**

## 1. Purpose
A comprehensive, self‑contained energy system built around a single wood‑fired core. Converts one fire into:

- Space heat  
- Cooking heat  
- Domestic hot water  
- Wood gas  
- Mechanical power  
- Compressed air  
- Electrical energy  
- Mechanical storage  
- Thermal storage  

Housed in a dedicated outbuilding for safety and modularity.

## 2. System Overview
At the center is a high‑efficiency wood‑burning stove/boiler. Surrounding subsystems operate simultaneously or on demand:

- Primary firebox  
- Thermal battery  
- Wood gasifier  
- Heat‑driven engine  
- Mechanical selector  
- Compressed air system  
- Solar integration  
- Cascading energy recovery  

A flexible, resilient micro‑power plant.

## 3. Core Components

### 3.1 Wood‑Fired Stove / Boiler
Provides:
- Cooking surface  
- Radiant heat  
- Hot flue gases  
- Boiler function  

Primary heat source for all downstream systems.

### 3.2 Hot Water System & Thermal Battery
Large insulated tank (300–800 L) stores heat from:
- Water jacket  
- Flue heat exchanger  
- Engine waste heat  

Provides:
- Domestic hot water  
- Hydronic heating  
- Heat buffering  
- Slow‑release heat  

### 3.3 Wood Gasifier Module
Side‑mounted downdraft gasifier using waste heat.

Outputs:
- Wood gas (CO, H₂, CH₄)  
- Charcoal  

Uses:
- Secondary burner  
- Engine fuel  
- Cooking  
- Short‑term storage  

### 3.4 Heat‑Driven Engine
Two options:

**A. Stirling Engine (recommended)**  
- External combustion  
- Quiet  
- Low maintenance  

**B. Steam Engine (higher power)**  
- Uses boiler steam  
- Drives larger compressors  
- Requires more safety systems  

Converts heat into mechanical rotation.

## 4. Energy Allocation System (“Selector Knob”)
Routes engine output to:

### 4.1 Battery Charging Mode
Engine → Generator → Charge Controller → Battery Bank

### 4.2 Flywheel Charging Mode
Engine → Flywheel via belt/clutch

### 4.3 Compressed Air Mode
Engine → Compressor → Tanks

### 4.4 Dual‑Mode Operation
Two systems can run simultaneously when fire intensity allows.

## 5. Cascading Energy Recovery
1. Primary heat  
2. Wood gas capture  
3. Engine mechanical output  
4. Engine waste heat  
5. Charcoal recovery  
6. Exhaust heat  

Near‑total energy utilization.

## 6. Compressed Air System
Engine drives compressor. Air stored in steel or composite tanks.

Uses:
- Pneumatic tools  
- Pumps  
- Air motors  
- Emergency power  

## 7. Energy Shed
Fire‑resistant structure (concrete, brick, steel).

Benefits:
- Keeps CO outside home  
- Isolates fire and pressure systems  
- Reduces noise  
- Simplifies venting  
- Allows modular expansion  

## 8. Solar Integration
Solar provides:
- Daytime charging  
- Silent baseline power  

Wood provides:
- Nighttime power  
- Winter power  
- Heavy‑load power  

## 9. Distribution to Home
### 9.1 Hot Water Line
Insulated PEX or copper.

### 9.2 Compressed Air Line
Buried or insulated line to workshop/garage.

## 10. Safety Considerations
- Sealed gasifier  
- CO detectors  
- Pressure relief valves  
- Fireproof construction  
- Proper chimney draft  

## 11. Benefits
- Local renewable fuel  
- Multi‑output energy  
- Resilient  
- Modular  
- EMP‑proof  
- Solar‑compatible  

## 12. Conclusion
A wood‑powered micro‑utility capable of supporting a modern home with minimal external inputs.

## 🌡️ Moisture Meter Summary
- Pin‑type meter recommended  
- Split log → measure fresh face  
- Ideal moisture: 15–20%  
- 30%+ too wet  
- Dry wood = hotter burns, cleaner combustion

## 🏠 Mackinac Island Case Study

### Heating
- Boiler output: 25–40 kW peak  
- Thermal battery: 18–47 kWh usable  

### Electricity
- Wood: ~6–12 kWh/day  
- Solar: ~4–8 kWh/day  
- Combined: ~10–20 kWh/day  

### Mechanical & Air
- 1–3 kW mechanical  
- 3–6 CFM at 90 psi  
- 1–3 kWh stored in air tanks  

### Footprint
- 10×12 or 12×16 ft shed  
- 8–9 ft ceiling  
- 500–800 L tank  
- Stove, engine, compressor, batteries
