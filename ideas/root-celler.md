# Root Cellar
*A passive, earth‑insulated food storage pattern for household and village‑scale resilience.*

## Purpose
A root cellar provides cool, stable, humidity‑controlled storage for produce, preserves, and dry goods without electricity. It uses the thermal inertia of the earth to maintain temperatures ideal for long‑term storage.

## Core Principles
- **Earth insulation** moderates temperature year‑round  
- **High humidity** prevents produce dehydration  
- **Ventilation** controls condensation and ethylene buildup  
- **Low light** slows sprouting and spoilage  
- **Simple shelving geometry** maximizes usable volume  

## Architectural Pattern
- Semi‑buried or fully earth‑sheltered  
- North‑facing entrance preferred  
- Double‑door or air‑lock vestibule for temperature stability  
- Smooth, level floor for accessibility  
- Shelving heights between 18–48 inches for universal reach  

## Environmental Behavior
- **Winter:** Earth moderates freezing temperatures  
- **Summer:** Earth moderates heat, maintaining cool interior  
- **Year‑round:** Slow thermal drift, predictable storage conditions  

## Suitable Storage
- root vegetables  
- apples and pears  
- canned goods  
- grains and dry goods  
- ferments and crocks  

## Maintenance
- seasonal ventilation adjustment  
- periodic cleaning  
- monitoring for condensation or pests  

## Integration Potential
Pairs naturally with:
- greenhouses  
- icehouses  
- earth‑sheltered corridors  
- household food‑processing workflows
