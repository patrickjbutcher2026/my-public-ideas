# Windhouse  
*A Legible, Safe‑Failure, Community‑Operated Mechanical Power House for a 150‑Person Village*

---

## Summary
The Windhouse is a centralized mechanical power station designed for a small community that values resilience, repairability, and shared stewardship. Instead of distributing electricity, it distributes **mechanical power**—torque and rotation—through belts, shafts, clutches, and flywheels.

It is not a high‑tech machine.  
It is a **village‑scale utility**, built to last generations, operated by ritual, and maintained through shared knowledge.

The Windhouse is both a **machine** and a **cultural institution**.

---

# 1. Doctrine of the Windhouse

## 1.1 Mechanical Legibility
All power flow must be visible, audible, and intuitive.  
No hidden logic. No black boxes.

## 1.2 Safe Failure
When something goes wrong, it must fail softly:  
belts slip, clutches disengage, blades feather.

## 1.3 Human Override
Operators retain final authority.  
Automation may assist, but never replace judgment.

## 1.4 Functional Redundancy
Twin rotors, segmented shafts, overflow storage, and manual/animal fallback ensure continuity.

## 1.5 Attention Efficiency
One building, one skill set, one place to maintain.

## 1.6 Cultural Redundancy
Knowledge is preserved through roles, apprenticeships, rituals, and seasonal practice.

## 1.7 Embodied Constraints
Unsafe states are physically prevented, not merely discouraged.

# 2. System Overview

## 2.1 Roof Level — The Power Suite
- Rotor A — high‑solidity torque rotor  
- Rotor B — low‑solidity speed rotor  
- Independent vertical shafts  
- Pitch controls and governors  
- Passive overspeed limiter  
- Segmented, contained flywheel  

## 2.2 Ground Level — The Workshop
- Main line shaft (compartmentalized)  
- Belt shifters and clutches  
- Milling, pumping, forging, sawing, machining, textiles  
- Overflow systems (gravity battery, cistern)  
- Mechanical startup sequencer  
- Painted slip zones on pulleys  
- External drive point for manual/animal backup  

---

# 3. Redundant Wind Engines

## Rotor A — Torque Engine
Starts the system in low wind.

## Rotor B — Velocity Engine
Adds power once the flywheel is spinning.

## Engagement Protection
- centrifugal clutch  
- governor‑linked lever  
- mechanical detent  

## Passive Overspeed Limiter
A visible, audible, manually resettable device enforcing maximum safe RPM.

---

# 4. Power Smoothing and Safety

## 4.1 Flywheel Safety
- segmented rim  
- iron burst ring  
- timber/earth containment wall  
- oversized bearings  
- sacrificial shear key  
- marked maximum RPM  

## 4.2 Shaft Compartmentalization
Each shaft segment includes:
- shear pin or friction clutch  
- alignment marks  
- isolation levers  

Prevents cascading failures.

# 10. Storm Mode

A ritualized configuration for high‑wind events:

- rotors feathered  
- flywheel disengaged  
- overspeed limiter armed  
- brake latched  
- overflow engaged  
- Tier 3 locked out  
- indicator wheel set to “storm mode”  

Practiced seasonally.
