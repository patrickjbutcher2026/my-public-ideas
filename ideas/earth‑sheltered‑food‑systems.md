# Earth‑Sheltered Food Systems
*A pattern for combining multiple passive food‑production and storage structures into a unified, climate‑stable complex.*

## Purpose
Earth‑sheltered food systems integrate greenhouses, root cellars, and icehouses into a single excavation and thermal envelope. This reduces construction effort, improves climate stability, and creates efficient workflows.

## Core Principles
- **Shared earthworks** reduce labor and materials  
- **Thermal zoning** places warm, cool, and cold spaces in sequence  
- **Dual access** supports daily use and seasonal loading  
- **Accessibility** ensures all spaces are usable by all residents  
- **Passive climate control** replaces mechanical systems  

## Typical Components
- Walipini or earth‑bermed greenhouse  
- Root cellar (cool zone)  
- Rammed‑earth icehouse (cold zone)  
- Shared corridor or access spine  
- Unified drainage and moisture management  

## Spatial Logic
Warm → Cool → Cold  
(greenhouse → root cellar → icehouse)

This gradient uses natural heat flow to stabilize all three spaces.

## Accessibility Pattern
- level thresholds  
- wide, smooth corridors  
- reachable shelving and planting beds  
- redundant exits for safety  

## Integration Benefits
- reduced excavation cost  
- improved thermal performance  
- efficient harvest‑to‑storage workflow  
- year‑round food resilience  
- minimal long‑term maintenance  

## Example Configurations
- household‑scale triad  
- village‑scale food hub  
- community emergency‑storage complex
