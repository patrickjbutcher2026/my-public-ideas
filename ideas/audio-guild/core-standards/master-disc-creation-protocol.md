# Master Disc Creation Protocol  
**Version 1.0 — Guild Reference Artifact Standard**

The Master Disc is the foundational reference object of the Audio Guild.  
It is the **meter bar**, the **tuning fork**, and the **Rosetta stone** of the entire audio ecosystem.  
Every settlement must maintain one, protect it, and use it to re‑establish precision whenever drift accumulates.

This document defines the complete process for creating, validating, storing, and replacing a Master Disc.

---

# 1. Purpose of the Master Disc

The Master Disc provides:

- speed reference  
- tonal reference  
- stylus geometry verification  
- groove depth calibration  
- apprentice training material  
- inter‑settlement compatibility  
- long‑term cultural continuity  

It is **never** used for entertainment.  
It is a **sacred engineering artifact**.

---

# 2. Physical Construction Requirements

## 2.1 Substrate  
The disc must be:

- **metal‑backed** (iron, brass, or bronze)  
- flat within **±0.1 mm**  
- resistant to warping  
- capable of surviving centuries if stored properly  

## 2.2 Coating Layer  
The recording surface must be:

- high‑purity shellac  
- reinforced with fine slate flour  
- free of fibers  
- poured and pressed under controlled temperature  

The coating must be **thicker** than a normal record to allow deep, stable calibration grooves.

## 2.3 Dimensions  
- Diameter: **10–12 inches**  
- Thickness: **5–7 mm**  
- Center hole: **standard Guild spindle size**  
- Edge: chamfered to prevent chipping  

---

# 3. Cutting Environment Requirements

The cutting room must be:

- dust‑free  
- low vibration  
- temperature‑stable  
- humidity‑controlled (as much as possible)  

Lighting must be bright and angled to reveal groove geometry.

The cutter lathe must be:

- freshly lubricated  
- speed‑verified  
- stylus‑verified  
- free of belt slip  

---

# 4. Cutting Stylus Requirements

The stylus must be:

- **sapphire or ruby**  
- polished to mirror finish  
- heated if settlement capability allows  
- mounted in a rigid, resonance‑free holder  

A reference stylus (sealed in wax) must be used to verify geometry before cutting.

---

# 5. Content of the Master Disc

The disc contains **four bands**, each with a specific purpose.

---

## 5.1 Band I — Speed Reference Tone  
A steady tone at:

- **440 Hz** (preferred)  
or  
- **A settlement‑specific reference tone**, logged in the Guild ledger  

Purpose:

- verify platter speed  
- tune governors  
- train apprentices in pitch‑matching  

---

## 5.2 Band II — Frequency Sweep  
A continuous sweep from:

- **100 Hz → 5,000 Hz**

Cut at constant amplitude.

Purpose:

- verify equalization  
- detect resonance peaks  
- identify stylus wear  
- check horn throat behavior  

---

## 5.3 Band III — Calibration Tones  
Steady tones at:

- 250 Hz  
- 500 Hz  
- 1,000 Hz  
- 2,000 Hz  
- 4,000 Hz  

Each tone lasts 5–10 seconds.

Purpose:

- check tonal balance  
- measure drift  
- compare machines across settlements  

---

## 5.4 Band IV — Spoken Calibration Phrases  
Recorded by a trained Guild Speaker.

Phrases include:

- “Guild Reference Phrase One.”  
- “S‑F‑T‑K consonant sequence.”  
- “Speed verification phrase.”  
- “Tone balance phrase.”  

Purpose:

- verify intelligibility  
- train apprentices  
- detect distortion  
- check groove geometry  

---

# 6. Cutting Procedure

## 6.1 Pre‑Cut Checklist  
- stylus geometry verified  
- platter speed stabilized  
- governor tuned  
- lathe bearings cleaned  
- shellac surface warmed  
- cutting head balanced  

## 6.2 Cutting Steps  
1. Begin with Band I (speed tone).  
2. Cut Band II (frequency sweep) in a single continuous pass.  
3. Cut Band III (steady tones).  
4. Cut Band IV (spoken phrases).  
5. Inspect grooves under magnification.  
6. Measure groove depth using reference gauge.  
7. Play back using a **non‑reference stylus** to avoid wear.  

## 6.3 Acceptance Criteria  
The disc is accepted only if:

- sweep is smooth  
- tones are stable  
- speech is intelligible  
- no chatter or tearing  
- groove depth within tolerance  
- no visible defects  

If any defect is found, the disc is destroyed and the process restarts.

---

# 7. Sealing & Storage

## 7.1 Sealing  
The accepted Master Disc must be:

- coated lightly with protective wax  
- wrapped in oiled cloth  
- placed in a sealed clay or metal container  
- labeled with date, settlement, and cutter initials  

## 7.2 Storage  
Store in:

- cool, dry, shaded location  
- underground vault if available  
- vibration‑free environment  

The disc must be stored **vertically**.

---

# 8. Replacement & Revalidation

## 8.1 Replacement  
A Master Disc may only be replaced:

- by order of the central Guild Hall  
- after catastrophic damage  
- after measurable drift beyond tolerance  
- after 20–30 years of service  

## 8.2 Revalidation  
Every **five years**, the disc must be:

- played on a calibrated machine  
- compared to a traveling Guild reference disc  
- inspected for wear  
- re‑sealed if necessary  

---

# 9. Apprentice Training Use  
The Master Disc is used to teach:

- pitch matching  
- drift detection  
- stylus inspection  
- tonal balance judgment  
- groove reading  

Apprentices must never handle the disc directly.

---

# 10. Conclusion  
The Master Disc is the anchor of the Guild’s entire audio ecosystem.  
It restores precision when tools drift, materials vary, and decades pass.  
It ensures that speech, music, and memory remain stable across settlements and generations.

A settlement without a Master Disc is a settlement without a voice.
