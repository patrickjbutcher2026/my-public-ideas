# Survival‑Grade Audio System Proposal  
**Revision 2.1 — Guild Consolidated Standard**

This document defines the survival‑grade audio system used across all settlements.  
It is engineered under the doctrine that **failure is expected**, **precision is re‑established**, and **operators are part of the control loop**.

---

## 1. Foundational Assumptions

### 1.1 Failure Is Expected  
All components will drift, wear, warp, fatigue, misalign, and accumulate contamination.  
The system is designed around predictable degradation, not its elimination.

### 1.2 Precision Is Re‑Established, Not Maintained  
No petrochemical plastics.  
No micro‑machining.  
No stable supply chain.  
Precision is restored through simple, repeatable geometry and reference artifacts.

### 1.3 Consumables Are a Design Layer  
Wax masters, styli, bearings, and shellac discs are sacrificial.  
Their wear protects the system.

### 1.4 Operators Are Part of the Control Loop  
Audio systems must be tactile, legible, bias‑guided, and repairable by intuition.  
The operator is a stabilizing subsystem.

### 1.5 Village‑Scale Manufacturability  
All improvements must be achievable with:
- cast iron  
- simple glasswork  
- natural resins  
- hand‑wound coils  
- kilns  
- forges  
- line‑shaft workshops  

---

## 2. Purpose of Revision 2.1  
This revision strengthens long‑term stability of shellac‑record production and playback by addressing:

- groove drift  
- speed drift  
- stylus inconsistency  
- resin variability  
- inter‑settlement incompatibility  
- cumulative distortion  

It introduces reference artifacts, guild rituals, and fallback systems to maintain coherence across decades.

---

## 3. New Requirements Introduced in Revision 2.1

### 3.1 Guild Master Disc System (Critical)  
Each settlement must maintain a protected Master Disc containing:

- frequency sweep  
- steady calibration tones  
- spoken calibration phrases  
- speed‑reference tone  

**Properties:**  
- metal‑backed  
- sealed storage  
- never used for entertainment  
- replaced only by the central Guild Hall  

**Purpose:**  
- calibrate cutter depth  
- verify playback speed  
- detect drift  
- train apprentices  

This is the **meter bar** of the audio ecosystem.

---

### 3.2 Shellac Compound Standardization  
To reduce resin variability:

- carbon black must be sieved to a defined particle size  
- lampblack may substitute for consistency  
- mixing must follow a time‑and‑motion protocol  
- resin lots must be labeled and logged  
- stylus hardness must remain softer than the filler  

This prevents abrasive wear and improves uniformity.

---

### 3.3 Stylus Geometry Standard  
Stylus shapes are defined by geometry, not terminology.

- **Conical:** tip radius ≈ thickness of two human hairs  
- **Elliptical:** verified using a metal gauge plate  
- **Reference stylus:** sealed in wax  
- **Standard hardness:** tempered steel  

This prevents stylus‑induced distortion drift.

---

### 3.4 Speed Calibration Standards  
Speed must be tunable by both eye and ear.

- strobe markings on all platters  
- tuning tone on Master Disc  
- audible pitch‑matching fallback  
- monthly speed checks  

This stabilizes pitch and reduces wow/flutter.

---

### 3.5 Equalization Curve Implementation (GRC‑1)  
The Guild Recording Curve must be implemented physically.

**Electrical:**  
- standard coil + capacitor network  
- defined turn counts  
- wire gauge  
- winding method  

**Mechanical fallback:**  
- felt pad treble damping  

This ensures tonal consistency across settlements.

---

### 3.6 Bearing Lubrication Doctrine  
Approved lubricants:

- graphite  
- plant oils  
- tallow blends  

Includes:

- cleaning intervals  
- contamination removal  
- seasonal adjustments  

This stabilizes speed and reduces rumble.

---

### 3.7 Environmental Control  
Records must be stored:

- vertically  
- in cool, shaded spaces  
- ideally in clay or underground vaults  

Warping is the primary long‑term threat.

---

### 3.8 Acoustic Recording Fallback  
All settlements must maintain:

- horn‑based acoustic cutting systems  
- standardized horn geometry  
- wax master preparation for acoustic cutting  

This ensures continuity under electrical failure.

---

### 3.9 Dual‑Head Recording for Important Material  
Two cutter heads record simultaneously with slightly different settings.

Benefits:

- one copy ages better  
- natural A/B comparison  
- reduced cumulative distortion  

---

### 3.10 Monthly Calibration Day Ritual  
Once per month:

- playback devices checked  
- stylus inspection  
- speed tuning  
- Master Disc verification  
- shared listening session  

This spreads tacit knowledge and prevents drift.
