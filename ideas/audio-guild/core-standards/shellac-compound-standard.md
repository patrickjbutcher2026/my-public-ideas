# Shellac Compound Standard — Version 1.1  
**Audio Guild — Materials & Resin Division**

This document defines the standardized shellac compound used for record production across all settlements.  
It ensures predictable wear, stable groove geometry, and long‑term inter‑settlement compatibility.

---

## 1. Purpose  
The Shellac Compound Standard establishes a **village‑manufacturable**, **repeatable**, and **diagnosable** formula suitable for:

- civic announcements  
- music  
- televisor soundtracks  
- archival recordings  
- inter‑settlement exchange  

Batch‑Alpha is optimized for **intelligibility**, **durability**, and **predictable degradation**, not high fidelity.

---

## 2. Batch‑Alpha Shellac Formula (Standard Mix)

| Ingredient | Proportion | Purpose |
|-----------|------------|---------|
| Refined Flake Shellac | 25% | Binder, surface hardness |
| Fine Slate Flour (Pass‑200 mesh) | 60% | Structural body, groove stability |
| Carbon Black (sieved lamp soot) | 10% | Lubrication, static reduction, opacity |
| Cotton Flock / Natural Fiber | 5% | Tensile strength, crack resistance |

### 2.1 Ingredient Notes  
- **Shellac:** Must be flake‑refined, free of bark and insect fragments.  
- **Slate Flour:** Must pass a **200‑mesh sieve**; coarse particles cause stylus chatter.  
- **Carbon Black:** Must be sieved; clumps cause groove tearing.  
- **Fibers:** Must be short, clean, and evenly dispersed.

---

## 3. Mixing Protocol (Mandatory)

### 3.1 Heating  
- Warm shellac gently until pliable.  
- Do **not** exceed temperatures that cause bubbling or scorching.  
- Maintain steady heat using charcoal, alcohol lamp, or kiln‑adjacent warming shelf.

### 3.2 Incorporation  
Add ingredients in the following order:

1. **Slate flour** — added slowly while stirring.  
2. **Carbon black** — sifted through a fine sieve.  
3. **Fibers** — added last to avoid clumping.

### 3.3 Mixing Duration  
- Mix for **12–15 minutes** at stable temperature.  
- Stirring must be continuous and rhythmic.  
- Avoid introducing air.

### 3.4 Degassing  
- Allow mixture to rest until surface bubbles collapse.  
- Tap container gently to release trapped air.  
- Proper degassing prevents micro‑voids in the groove walls.

---

## 4. Resin Diagnostics

### 4.1 Visual Inspection  
A correct batch should appear:

- uniformly black  
- matte, not glossy  
- free of streaks or clumps  
- smooth when pressed between fingers (after cooling)

### 4.2 Bend Test  
A cooled sample should:

- flex slightly before cracking  
- break cleanly, not crumble  
- show no white stress lines

### 4.3 Groove Test  
Cut a shallow test groove using a reference stylus:

- walls should be smooth  
- no tearing or chatter  
- stylus should not squeal  
- debris should be fine and powdery

---

## 5. Stylus Hardness Requirements  
To prevent abrasive wear:

- stylus must always be **softer** than the filler (slate flour).  
- standard stylus hardness: **tempered steel**  
- sapphire or ruby may be used only for **cutting**, never for playback.

---

## 6. Resin Lot Logging  
Every batch must be logged with:

- date  
- resin source  
- slate flour source  
- carbon black source  
- fiber source  
- operator initials  
- mixing duration  
- temperature notes  

This enables long‑term drift tracking and inter‑settlement compatibility.

---

## 7. Storage & Handling  
- Store raw shellac in sealed clay jars.  
- Keep carbon black dry and sifted.  
- Protect slate flour from moisture.  
- Keep fibers clean and pest‑free.  
- Finished records must be stored vertically in cool, shaded spaces.
