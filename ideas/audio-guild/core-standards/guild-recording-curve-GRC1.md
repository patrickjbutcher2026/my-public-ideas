# Guild Recording Curve (GRC‑1)  
**Version 1.1 — Technical Addendum**

The Guild Recording Curve (GRC‑1) is the standardized pre‑emphasis and de‑emphasis system used across all settlements.  
It maximizes intelligibility under survival‑grade manufacturing constraints and ensures tonal compatibility between settlements.

---

## 1. Purpose  
GRC‑1 exists to:

- bury shellac surface noise beneath boosted high‑frequency content  
- restore natural tonal balance during playback  
- maintain inter‑settlement compatibility  
- provide a predictable reference for apprentices and recordists  
- reduce cumulative distortion across generations of duplication  

The curve is designed for **village manufacturability**, **mechanical fallback**, and **long‑term stability**.

---

## 2. Recording Curve (Pre‑Emphasis)

### 2.1 Standard Slope  
A fixed **+6 dB per octave** boost beginning at **2,500 Hz**.

### 2.2 Tolerance Band  
- Acceptable slope range: **+5 to +7 dB/octave**  
- Acceptable start frequency: **2,000–3,000 Hz**  
- Deviations must be logged in the settlement’s calibration ledger.

### 2.3 Rationale  
Shellac surface noise is strongest in the high frequencies.  
Boosting highs during recording ensures:

- hiss is masked  
- consonants remain intelligible  
- music retains clarity  
- televisor soundtracks remain decipherable  

This is especially critical for **speech**, the primary civic use case.

---

## 3. Playback Curve (De‑Emphasis)

### 3.1 Standard Slope  
A matching **–6 dB per octave** cut beginning at the same turnover frequency.

### 3.2 Implementation  
Playback systems must include:

- a standardized coil + capacitor network  
- defined turn counts  
- defined wire gauge  
- defined winding method  
- mechanical damping fallback

### 3.3 Mechanical Fallback  
If electrical components fail or drift:

- a felt damping pad may be applied to the stylus mount  
- a mass‑loaded stylus arm may be used to attenuate high frequencies  
- horn throat inserts may be used to reduce treble energy  

Mechanical fallback ensures continuity during electrical outages.

---

## 4. Electrical Implementation Details

### 4.1 Coil Specifications  
- Hand‑wound  
- Soft iron core  
- Standardized turn count (per Guild tables)  
- Wire gauge: **28–32 AWG equivalent**  
- Must be sealed with natural resin to prevent moisture drift

### 4.2 Capacitor Specifications  
- Wax‑paper or glass‑dielectric  
- Capacitance tolerance: **±10%**  
- Must be tested annually using the Master Disc

### 4.3 Network Configuration  
A simple RC or LC network is used depending on settlement capability:

- **RC network:** easier to build, less stable over decades  
- **LC network:** more stable, requires better materials  

Both are acceptable if they fall within the GRC‑1 tolerance band.

---

## 5. Verification Using the Master Disc  
The Master Disc contains:

- a high‑frequency sweep  
- steady tones at octave intervals  
- spoken calibration phrases  
- a reference treble‑rich consonant test (“s‑f‑t‑k sequence”)  

Playback must be checked for:

- tonal balance  
- excessive hiss  
- muffled consonants  
- over‑bright playback  
- resonance peaks  

Any deviation must be corrected before public use.

---

## 6. Drift & Failure Modes

### 6.1 Electrical Drift  
- coil resistance increases with corrosion  
- capacitor value drifts with humidity  
- solder joints oxidize  
- wire insulation absorbs moisture  

### 6.2 Mechanical Drift  
- felt pads compress  
- stylus mounts loosen  
- horn throats accumulate dust  
- damping materials stiffen with age  

### 6.3 Resin & Shellac Drift  
- high‑frequency loss due to groove wear  
- carbon black distribution changes with batch variability  

GRC‑1 is designed to compensate for these predictable degradations.

---

## 7. Inter‑Settlement Compatibility  
All settlements must:

- implement GRC‑1 within tolerance  
- verify playback using the Master Disc  
- log deviations  
- train apprentices using the same tonal references  

This ensures that a record cut in one settlement plays correctly in another, even decades later.

---

## 8. Expected Outcomes  
- reduced surface noise  
- improved intelligibility  
- consistent tonal balance  
- stable long‑term playback  
- predictable degradation  
- compatibility across all Guild settlements  

---

## 9. Conclusion  
GRC‑1 is the backbone of the Guild’s audio ecosystem.  
It is simple, durable, and designed for a world where materials drift, tools wear, and precision must be re‑established through ritual and reference.

It ensures that **speech remains clear**, **music remains recognizable**, and **culture remains transmissible** across generations.
