# Speed Governor Standard  
**Audio Guild — Mechanical Stability Division**  
**Version 1.0**

This document defines the construction, calibration, maintenance, and drift‑detection procedures for platter speed governors used in Guild‑approved playback and cutting machines.  
Stable rotational speed is essential for intelligibility, tonal accuracy, and inter‑settlement compatibility.

The governor is the **heartbeat regulator** of the entire audio ecosystem.

---

# 1. Purpose

The Speed Governor Standard ensures:

- stable platter rotation  
- predictable pitch  
- reduced wow and flutter  
- consistent playback across settlements  
- reliable cutting speed for Master Discs and archival records  
- long‑term drift detection and correction  

Without a properly calibrated governor, **all other standards fail**.

---

# 2. Governor Types Recognized by the Guild

The Guild recognizes three governor designs:

1. **Flyball Governor** — preferred; most stable  
2. **Friction‑Pad Governor** — acceptable; easier to build  
3. **Flywheel‑Assisted Governor** — optional upgrade; highest stability  

All must meet the same performance tolerances.

---

# 3. Performance Requirements

## 3.1 Target Speed  
Standard playback and cutting speed:

- **85–100 rpm**, settlement‑dependent  
- exact speed must be logged in the Speed Ledger  
- Master Disc tone must match chosen speed  

## 3.2 Tolerances  
- short‑term flutter: **≤ 1%**  
- long‑term drift: **≤ 2% per month**  
- startup overshoot: **≤ 5%**  

## 3.3 Stability Under Load  
Governor must maintain speed when:

- stylus enters groove  
- horn back‑pressure changes  
- platter temperature shifts  
- lubrication thins or thickens  

---

# 4. Flyball Governor Standard (Preferred)

## 4.1 Components  
- two or three steel balls  
- pivoting arms  
- central spindle  
- adjustable tension spring  
- friction collar or brake pad  

## 4.2 Operation  
As platter speed increases:

- balls swing outward  
- arms lift  
- collar engages brake  
- speed stabilizes  

## 4.3 Construction Requirements  
- arms must be symmetrical  
- balls must be equal mass  
- spring must be tempered  
- collar must be smooth and clean  

## 4.4 Calibration  
1. Spin platter by hand.  
2. Observe ball lift height.  
3. Adjust spring tension until lift matches reference mark.  
4. Verify using Master Disc 440 Hz tone.  
5. Log final tension setting.

---

# 5. Friction‑Pad Governor Standard

## 5.1 Components  
- rotating disc  
- adjustable friction pad  
- tension screw  

## 5.2 Operation  
Pad applies increasing resistance as speed rises.

## 5.3 Construction Requirements  
- pad must be leather, felt, or resin‑treated cloth  
- friction surface must be clean  
- tension screw must turn smoothly  

## 5.4 Calibration  
1. Tighten pad until platter slows audibly.  
2. Loosen until speed rises above target.  
3. Adjust to midpoint.  
4. Verify with Master Disc tone.  

---

# 6. Flywheel‑Assisted Governor (Optional Upgrade)

## 6.1 Purpose  
Flywheel increases rotational inertia, reducing:

- wow  
- flutter  
- stylus‑induced drag  

## 6.2 Requirements  
- flywheel mass must be balanced  
- bearings must be high quality  
- shaft must be straight  

## 6.3 Calibration  
Same as flyball governor, but with slower response time.

---

# 7. Calibration Procedure (Monthly)

Performed during Calibration Day.

### Step 1 — Warm‑Up  
- run platter for 3–5 minutes  
- allow lubricants to stabilize  

### Step 2 — Master Disc Tone  
- play 440 Hz reference tone  
- adjust governor until pitch matches reference  

### Step 3 — Strobe Verification  
- use strobe markings under flickering lamp  
- confirm visual stability  

### Step 4 — Load Test  
- lower stylus into groove  
- ensure speed does not sag  

### Step 5 — Log Results  
- record final tension setting  
- note any drift from previous month  

---

# 8. Drift Detection

## 8.1 Audible Signs  
- pitch wobble  
- flutter on sustained notes  
- speech “waver”  
- inconsistent consonants  

## 8.2 Mechanical Signs  
- uneven ball lift  
- sticky collar  
- worn friction pad  
- loose tension screw  
- dry bearings  

## 8.3 Logging  
All drift must be recorded in:

- Speed Ledger  
- Machine Drift Report  

---

# 9. Maintenance

## 9.1 Lubrication  
Approved lubricants:

- graphite  
- plant oils  
- tallow blends  

Apply monthly.

## 9.2 Cleaning  
- remove dust from arms and balls  
- wipe collar  
- clean friction surfaces  

## 9.3 Replacement  
Replace components if:

- balls become pitted  
- arms bend  
- spring loses tension  
- pad becomes glazed  

---

# 10. Emergency Procedures

If governor fails during playback:

1. stop machine immediately  
2. inspect for loose parts  
3. switch to backup machine if available  
4. postpone cutting operations  
5. duplicate critical records once stable  

---

# 11. Expected Outcomes

- stable pitch  
- reduced wow/flutter  
- consistent inter‑settlement playback  
- reliable cutting speed  
- predictable long‑term drift  
- safer playback of archival material
