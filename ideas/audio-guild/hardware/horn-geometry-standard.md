# Horn Geometry Standard  
**Audio Guild — Acoustic Engineering Division**  
**Version 1.0**

This document defines the standardized horn geometries used for Guild‑approved acoustic playback and acoustic cutting systems.  
Horn shape is the single most influential factor in **intelligibility**, **tonal balance**, and **mechanical efficiency** for non‑electrical audio systems.

The Horn Geometry Standard ensures that all settlements produce horns with predictable acoustic behavior, stable resonance characteristics, and long‑term inter‑settlement compatibility.

---

# 1. Purpose

The Horn Geometry Standard exists to:

- stabilize tonal balance across settlements  
- ensure clear speech reproduction  
- minimize “honk,” ringing, and throat turbulence  
- provide predictable resonance behavior  
- support both playback and acoustic cutting  
- enable calibration using the Acoustic Calibration Bridge  
- maintain compatibility with GRC‑1 tonal expectations  

A horn built to this standard will produce **intelligible, stable, and culturally consistent sound**.

---

# 2. Horn Types Recognized by the Guild

The Guild recognizes three horn geometries:

1. **Conical Horn** — simplest, most stable  
2. **Exponential Horn** — best tonal balance  
3. **Hybrid Horn** — conical throat + exponential body  

All three are acceptable if built within tolerance.

---

# 3. Materials

Approved materials:

- hardwood (maple, ash, oak)  
- sheet metal (tin, brass, steel)  
- clay or ceramic  
- composite (wood throat + metal flare)  

Material affects resonance but not geometry requirements.

---

# 4. Dimensional Standards

## 4.1 Throat Diameter  
- **8–15 mm**  
- must be smooth and free of steps  
- smaller throats increase back‑pressure  
- larger throats reduce efficiency  

## 4.2 Horn Length  
- **300–600 mm**  
- longer horns improve low‑mid response  
- shorter horns improve portability  

## 4.3 Mouth Diameter  
- **150–300 mm**  
- larger mouths reduce coloration  
- smaller mouths increase projection  

## 4.4 Wall Thickness  
- wood: **5–10 mm**  
- metal: **0.5–1.5 mm**  
- clay: **5–12 mm**  

Thicker walls reduce ringing.

---

# 5. Geometry Specifications

## 5.1 Conical Horn (Standard)

### Shape  
- straight‑line expansion  
- constant flare angle  

### Acoustic Behavior  
- strong midrange  
- slight high‑frequency emphasis  
- minimal coloration  

### Use Cases  
- playback devices  
- field recorders  
- apprentice‑built horns  

---

## 5.2 Exponential Horn (Preferred)

### Shape  
Follows the exponential flare equation:

- area increases exponentially along length  
- smooth, natural expansion  

### Acoustic Behavior  
- best tonal balance  
- reduced “honk”  
- improved low‑mid clarity  
- smoother consonants  

### Use Cases  
- archival playback  
- acoustic cutting  
- high‑quality listening devices  

---

## 5.3 Hybrid Horn (Optional)

### Shape  
- conical throat (first 50–100 mm)  
- exponential body  

### Acoustic Behavior  
- stable throat behavior  
- smooth overall response  
- reduced turbulence  

### Use Cases  
- settlements with mixed materials  
- horns built from multiple segments  

---

# 6. Throat Requirements

The throat is the most critical part of the horn.

## 6.1 Smoothness  
- no ridges  
- no steps  
- no tool marks  
- no glue beads  

## 6.2 Alignment  
- throat must align perfectly with diaphragm chamber  
- misalignment causes distortion and turbulence  

## 6.3 Reinforcement  
- wood throats may be lined with metal  
- metal throats may be stiffened with ribs  

---

# 7. Resonance Control

## 7.1 Anti‑Ringing Measures  
- apply resin or wax coating inside wood horns  
- add felt pads to metal horns  
- use thicker walls for clay horns  

## 7.2 Standing Wave Reduction  
- avoid perfectly cylindrical sections  
- ensure smooth flare transitions  
- avoid sudden diameter changes  

## 7.3 Damping  
- external leather straps  
- internal felt patches (sparingly)  
- sand‑filled outer ribs (optional)  

---

# 8. Acoustic Calibration Procedure

Using the **Acoustic Calibration Bridge**:

1. excite low notch → check low‑mid resonance  
2. excite mid notch → check throat behavior  
3. excite high notch → check treble clarity  
4. listen for:  
   - buzzing  
   - ringing  
   - honk  
   - turbulence  
   - uneven response  

Any deviation must be logged and corrected.

---

# 9. Construction Guidelines

## 9.1 Wood Horns  
- carve from solid block or assemble from staves  
- seal interior with resin  
- avoid knots and cracks  

## 9.2 Metal Horns  
- form from sheet metal  
- solder seams smoothly  
- reinforce mouth with rolled edge  

## 9.3 Clay/Ceramic Horns  
- coil‑built or wheel‑thrown  
- fire at stable temperature  
- glaze interior lightly  

---

# 10. Failure Modes & Remedies

| Failure Mode | Cause | Remedy |
|--------------|-------|--------|
| Honk | poor flare geometry | re‑shape or sand interior |
| Ringing | thin walls | add damping or reinforce |
| Muffled sound | throat misalignment | re‑seat diaphragm chamber |
| Harsh treble | throat too narrow | widen throat slightly |
| Weak output | leaks or cracks | seal or repair |
