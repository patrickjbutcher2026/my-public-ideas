# Cutter Lathe Specification — Version 1.0  
**Audio Guild — Recording & Mastering Division**

This document defines the standardized cutter lathe used for producing Guild‑approved master discs, program discs, and archival duplicates.  
The design prioritizes **mechanical stability**, **repeatable geometry**, **village‑scale manufacturability**, and **predictable drift behavior**.

The cutter lathe is the most sensitive machine in the Guild’s ecosystem.  
Its precision determines the clarity, compatibility, and longevity of every record produced.

---

# 1. Purpose

The Guild Cutter Lathe (CL‑1) is designed to:

- cut stable, uniform grooves in Batch‑Alpha shellac  
- maintain consistent depth and width  
- operate without electricity (optional electrical assist allowed)  
- integrate with the Master Disc Creation Protocol  
- support both acoustic and electrical cutting heads  
- remain serviceable with basic tools  
- minimize chatter, tearing, and resonance  

CL‑1 is the **standard cutting platform** for all settlements.

---

# 2. System Overview

The CL‑1 consists of:

1. **Platter & Bearing Assembly**  
2. **Drive System (Spring, Weight, or Line‑Shaft)**  
3. **Speed Governor**  
4. **Cutting Head (Acoustic or Electrical)**  
5. **Stylus & Heating System**  
6. **Carriage & Leadscrew Assembly**  
7. **Chassis & Vibration Isolation**  

Each subsystem is defined below.

---

# 3. Platter & Bearing Assembly

## 3.1 Platter Material  
Approved materials:

- cast iron (preferred)  
- bronze  
- steel with damping ring  

## 3.2 Dimensions  
- Diameter: **12–14 inches**  
- Thickness: **10–15 mm**  
- Mass: **3–6 kg** (high mass improves groove stability)

## 3.3 Bearing System  
- hardened steel spindle  
- precision‑bored bronze sleeve  
- oil reservoir or wick system  
- dust cap or felt seal  

## 3.4 Flatness Requirement  
Platter must be flat within:

- **±0.05 mm** across full diameter  

Warped platters are prohibited.

---

# 4. Drive System

The CL‑1 supports three drive types:

1. **Spring Motor**  
2. **Falling Weight Drive**  
3. **Line‑Shaft Drive** (shared workshop power)

## 4.1 Requirements  
Regardless of drive type:

- torque must remain stable under cutting load  
- no belt slip allowed  
- drive must not introduce rumble or vibration  

## 4.2 Recommended Drive  
**Falling Weight Drive** is preferred for its:

- smooth torque curve  
- low vibration  
- easy maintenance  

---

# 5. Speed Governor

The governor must meet the **Speed Governor Standard**.

## 5.1 Target Speed  
- **85–100 rpm**, settlement‑specific  
- must match Master Disc reference tone  

## 5.2 Stability  
- flutter ≤ 1%  
- drift ≤ 2% per month  

---

# 6. Cutting Head

The CL‑1 supports two cutting modes:

1. **Acoustic Cutting Head**  
2. **Electrical Cutting Head** (if settlement capability allows)

---

## 6.1 Acoustic Cutting Head

### Components  
- diaphragm (mica, hardwood, or metal foil)  
- horn throat  
- stylus mount  
- damping pad  

### Requirements  
- diaphragm tension adjustable  
- horn throat smooth and clean  
- stylus mount rigid  
- no air leaks  

### Use Cases  
- fallback cutting  
- field recording  
- settlements without electrical capability  

---

## 6.2 Electrical Cutting Head

### Components  
- coil (hand‑wound)  
- iron armature  
- stylus mount  
- damping block  
- optional stylus heater  

### Requirements  
- coil resistance within ±10% of spec  
- armature centered  
- damping block flexible but firm  
- stylus heater stable and insulated  

### Use Cases  
- Master Disc creation  
- archival duplication  
- high‑clarity program discs  

---

# 7. Stylus & Heating System

## 7.1 Cutting Stylus  
- sapphire or ruby  
- mirror‑polished  
- mounted rigidly  
- replaceable  

## 7.2 Stylus Geometry  
- defined by Stylus Geometry Standard  
- elliptical or wedge‑shaped tip allowed for cutting  

## 7.3 Stylus Heating  
If available:

- heating coil wrapped around stylus shank  
- powered by low‑voltage source  
- temperature: warm enough to soften shellac, not scorch it  

Heating reduces:

- chatter  
- tearing  
- debris size  

---

# 8. Carriage & Leadscrew Assembly

## 8.1 Carriage  
- rides on hardened steel rails  
- must move smoothly with no play  
- adjustable counterweight  

## 8.2 Leadscrew  
- pitch: **1.0–1.5 mm per revolution**  
- must be straight and clean  
- driven by platter or separate gear train  

## 8.3 Groove Pitch Control  
Two modes:

1. **Fixed Pitch** — simplest, most reliable  
2. **Variable Pitch** — optional, requires skilled operator  

## 8.4 Anti‑Backlash Mechanism  
- spring‑loaded nut  
- prevents groove spacing irregularities  

---

# 9. Chassis & Vibration Isolation

## 9.1 Base Material  
- hardwood  
- cast iron  
- layered composite  

## 9.2 Isolation  
- rubber feet  
- felt pads  
- sand‑filled compartments (optional)  

## 9.3 Structural Requirements  
- rigid frame  
- no flex under cutting load  
- cutting head isolated from drive vibration  

---

# 10. Cutting Procedure (Standard)

1. warm shellac blank  
2. verify platter speed  
3. inspect stylus  
4. set groove pitch  
5. lower cutting head  
6. begin recording  
7. monitor debris and stylus sound  
8. lift head at end  
9. inspect groove under magnification  

---

# 11. Failure Modes & Remedies

| Failure Mode | Cause | Remedy |
|--------------|-------|--------|
| Chatter | stylus too cold or dull | heat stylus, replace tip |
| Groove tearing | resin too brittle | adjust resin mix |
| Wobble | governor drift | recalibrate |
| Rumble | dry bearings | lubricate |
| Uneven pitch | leadscrew backlash | adjust anti‑backlash nut |
| Distortion | diaphragm tension drift | retension diaphragm |

---

# 12. Maintenance

## Daily  
- clean stylus  
- dust rails  
- check diaphragm tension  

## Weekly  
- lubricate bearings  
- clean horn throat  
- inspect leadscrew  

## Monthly  
- full Calibration Day procedure  

## Annual  
- replace damping pads  
- re‑polish rails  
- inspect platter flatness
