# Resin Diagnostics  
**Audio Guild — Materials Division**  
**Version 1.0**

This document provides standardized diagnostic procedures for evaluating resin quality, batch consistency, and long‑term drift in shellac‑based record compounds.  
It is used by Recordists, Mixers, and Materials Stewards to ensure stable groove geometry and predictable playback behavior across all settlements.

---

# 1. Purpose

Resin diagnostics allow settlements to:

- detect bad batches before pressing  
- identify contamination sources  
- track long‑term drift in resin quality  
- maintain inter‑settlement compatibility  
- ensure stylus‑safe hardness levels  
- prevent groove tearing and chatter  

Diagnostics are performed **before pressing**, **after pressing**, and **during archival checks**.

---

# 2. Diagnostic Categories

Resin diagnostics fall into four groups:

1. **Visual Diagnostics** — color, texture, uniformity  
2. **Mechanical Diagnostics** — hardness, flexibility, fracture behavior  
3. **Thermal Diagnostics** — melt behavior, viscosity, temperature stability  
4. **Groove Diagnostics** — cutting behavior, debris type, stylus response  

Each category must be logged in the settlement’s Resin Ledger.

---

# 3. Visual Diagnostics

## 3.1 Color Uniformity  
A correct batch should be:

- uniformly black  
- matte, not glossy  
- free of streaks  
- free of visible fibers or clumps  

**Failure Indicators:**

- gray streaks → incomplete mixing  
- shiny patches → overheated shellac  
- brown tint → contaminated or old resin  
- visible grit → insufficient sieving of slate flour  

## 3.2 Surface Texture  
When cooled:

- surface should be smooth  
- no pitting  
- no bubbles  
- no waxy bloom  

**Failure Indicators:**

- pitting → trapped air (insufficient degassing)  
- bubbles → overheated resin  
- wax bloom → moisture contamination  

---

# 4. Mechanical Diagnostics

## 4.1 Hardness Test  
Press thumbnail into cooled sample:

- slight indentation = correct  
- deep indentation = too soft  
- no indentation = too hard (dangerous for stylus)

## 4.2 Bend Test  
Break a cooled strip:

- should flex slightly before cracking  
- fracture should be clean  
- no crumbling  
- no white stress lines  

## 4.3 Compression Test  
Press between two flat plates:

- edges should not crumble  
- surface should not flake  
- slight compression is acceptable  

---

# 5. Thermal Diagnostics

## 5.1 Melt Behavior  
Heat a small sample:

- should soften gradually  
- should not bubble  
- should not scorch  
- should not emit sharp odor  

**Failure Indicators:**

- bubbling → overheated during mixing  
- rapid melting → too much shellac  
- charring → contaminated resin  

## 5.2 Viscosity Check  
Stir heated resin:

- should flow smoothly  
- should not clump  
- should not string excessively  

## 5.3 Cooling Behavior  
As resin cools:

- should transition smoothly from liquid → pliable → solid  
- should not form a skin prematurely  

---

# 6. Groove Diagnostics

Groove diagnostics are the most important category.  
They determine whether the resin will cut cleanly and play back reliably.

## 6.1 Test Groove Cut  
Cut a shallow groove using a reference stylus:

- walls should be smooth  
- no tearing  
- no chatter  
- no squeal  

## 6.2 Debris Analysis  
Examine debris:

- fine powder = correct  
- flakes = resin too brittle  
- strings = resin too soft  
- shiny chips = slate flour too coarse  

## 6.3 Stylus Response  
Listen during cutting:

- steady hiss = normal  
- squeal = stylus too hard or resin too brittle  
- rumble = platter instability (log separately)  

## 6.4 Groove Wall Integrity  
Under magnification:

- walls should be uniform  
- no micro‑cracks  
- no voids  
- no embedded fibers  

---

# 7. Batch Drift Tracking

Each settlement must maintain a **Resin Drift Ledger** recording:

- resin source  
- slate flour source  
- carbon black source  
- fiber source  
- mixing duration  
- temperature notes  
- diagnostic results  
- operator initials  

Over years, this allows the Guild to:

- identify seasonal drift  
- detect contamination patterns  
- compare settlements  
- refine formulas  

---

# 8. Common Failure Modes & Remedies

| Failure Mode | Cause | Remedy |
|--------------|-------|--------|
| Groove tearing | coarse slate flour | re‑sieve flour |
| Stylus chatter | resin too brittle | increase shellac by 2–3% |
| Excess hiss | carbon black clumping | re‑sieve carbon black |
| Cracking | insufficient fibers | increase fiber content |
| Warping | moisture absorption | improve storage conditions |
| Dull playback | resin too soft | increase slate flour by 2–5% |

---

# 9. Archival Resin Checks

Every **five years**, archival discs must be inspected for:

- warping  
- surface bloom  
- groove whitening  
- brittleness  
- mold intrusion  
- delamination  

Any disc showing structural failure must be duplicated immediately.

# 10. Conclusion

Resin diagnostics are essential to the survival‑grade audio ecosystem.  
They ensure that every record—whether civic announcement or oral history—remains intelligible, durable, and compatible across settlements.

A settlement that neglects resin diagnostics will lose its voice long before its tools fail.
