# Stylus Geometry Standard  
**Audio Guild — Mechanical & Cutting Division**  
**Version 1.0**

This document defines the standardized geometries, hardness requirements, verification methods, and maintenance practices for all Guild‑approved playback and cutting styli.  
Consistent stylus geometry is essential for stable groove behavior, predictable wear, and inter‑settlement compatibility.

---

# 1. Purpose

The Stylus Geometry Standard ensures:

- consistent playback across settlements  
- predictable groove wear  
- reduced distortion  
- stable tracking in wide survival‑grade grooves  
- compatibility with Batch‑Alpha shellac  
- reliable calibration using the Master Disc  

Stylus drift is one of the most common causes of tonal imbalance, distortion, and groove damage.  
This standard prevents that drift from accumulating.

---

# 2. Stylus Types Recognized by the Guild

The Guild recognizes **two** primary stylus geometries:

1. **Conical Stylus** — default for playback  
2. **Elliptical Stylus** — optional for high‑clarity playback or cutting verification  

Both geometries must be defined by **measurable shape**, not terminology.

---

# 3. Conical Stylus Standard (Playback Default)

## 3.1 Tip Radius  
The conical stylus must have a tip radius approximately equal to:

- **the thickness of two human hairs**  
  (≈ **0.10–0.15 mm**)

This radius matches the wide, deep groove profile of survival‑grade shellac records.

## 3.2 Geometry Requirements  
- spherical tip  
- smooth curvature  
- no flats or facets  
- no visible asymmetry  

## 3.3 Material  
- **tempered steel** (standard)  
- iron or bronze acceptable for emergency use  
- sapphire/ruby **not allowed** for playback (too hard; damages grooves)

## 3.4 Use Cases  
- everyday playback  
- archival listening  
- apprentice training  
- Master Disc verification (with a *non‑reference* stylus)

---

# 4. Elliptical Stylus Standard (Optional)

## 4.1 Purpose  
The elliptical stylus provides:

- improved high‑frequency tracking  
- reduced inner‑groove distortion  
- better consonant clarity  

It is optional and settlement‑dependent.

## 4.2 Geometry Verification  
Elliptical styli must be verified using a **metal gauge plate** with:

- narrow slot for minor radius  
- wide slot for major radius  

If the stylus does not seat cleanly in both slots, it is rejected.

## 4.3 Material  
- tempered steel (preferred)  
- sapphire/ruby allowed **only for cutting**, not playback

## 4.4 Use Cases  
- cutting verification  
- high‑clarity playback  
- archival duplication  
- tonal drift analysis  

---

# 5. Cutting Stylus Standard

Cutting styli must be:

- **sapphire or ruby**  
- polished to a mirror finish  
- optionally heated (if settlement capability allows)  
- mounted in a rigid, resonance‑free holder  

Cutting styli must be stored separately from playback styli.

---

# 6. Stylus Hardness Requirements

To prevent groove damage:

- playback stylus must be **softer** than slate‑reinforced shellac  
- cutting stylus must be **harder** than shellac  

### Hardness Hierarchy  
1. **Sapphire/Ruby** — cutting only  
2. **Tempered Steel** — playback  
3. **Iron/Bronze** — emergency playback  

Never reverse this hierarchy.

---

# 7. Stylus Inspection Protocol

Styli must be inspected:

- **monthly** (regular use)  
- **before any archival playback**  
- **before Master Disc playback**  
- **after any audible distortion event**  

## 7.1 Inspection Tools  
- magnifying lens (10× minimum)  
- angled light source  
- metal gauge plate (for elliptical styli)  
- reference stylus (sealed in wax)

## 7.2 Inspection Criteria  
Reject stylus if:

- tip is flattened  
- tip is chipped  
- tip is asymmetrical  
- metal shows corrosion  
- stylus squeals during playback  
- stylus produces excessive debris  

---

# 8. Stylus Drift & Failure Modes

| Failure Mode | Cause | Effect | Remedy |
|--------------|-------|--------|--------|
| Flat spot | heavy use | distortion, hiss | re‑grind or replace |
| Chipped tip | impact | groove tearing | replace immediately |
| Asymmetry | poor sharpening | channel imbalance | re‑shape |
| Corrosion | moisture | noise, chatter | polish or replace |
| Over‑hard stylus | wrong material | groove damage | replace with tempered steel |

---

# 9. Sharpening & Re‑Shaping

Playback styli may be re‑shaped using:

- fine whetstone  
- leather strop  
- polishing compound  

Cutting styli **must not** be reshaped locally; they require Guild‑level facilities.

---

# 10. Storage & Handling

- store styli in waxed cloth or sealed wooden cases  
- keep cutting styli in separate labeled containers  
- avoid moisture and dust  
- never touch the tip with bare fingers  
- keep reference stylus sealed in wax at all times  

---

# 11. Conclusion

Stylus geometry is the most fragile link in the survival‑grade audio chain.  
By enforcing strict geometric standards, settlements maintain:

- predictable groove behavior  
- stable tonal balance  
- reduced distortion  
- long‑term compatibility  
- safe playback of archival material  

A stylus out of standard is a danger to every record it touches.
