# Calibration Day Ritual  
**Audio Guild — Monthly Standardized Procedure**  
**Version 1.0**

Calibration Day is the monthly ritual that prevents drift, spreads tacit knowledge, and keeps every settlement’s audio system aligned with Guild standards.  
It is both a **technical procedure** and a **cultural practice**, ensuring that precision is re‑established regularly rather than assumed.

This document defines the complete ritual, its purpose, its sequence, and its expected outcomes.

---

# 1. Purpose of Calibration Day

Calibration Day exists to:

- detect drift in playback and cutting systems  
- verify stylus geometry and wear  
- tune governors and stabilize platter speed  
- check tonal balance using the Master Disc  
- compare machines across operators  
- reinforce shared listening skills  
- maintain inter‑settlement compatibility  
- train apprentices through observation and participation  

It is the **heartbeat** of the Guild’s survival‑grade audio ecosystem.

---

# 2. Frequency & Participation

- Held **once per month**, ideally on the same day.  
- Attendance required for:  
  - Recordists  
  - Apprentices  
  - Materials Stewards  
  - Machine Tenders  
  - Archivists  
- Optional attendance for the general public (listening session only).

---

# 3. Preparation Phase

## 3.1 Cleaning & Workspace Setup  
Before the ritual begins:

- sweep workshop floors  
- dust all machines  
- clean horn throats  
- wipe platters  
- prepare magnifying lenses  
- warm shellac samples for resin checks  
- lay out stylus inspection tools  

## 3.2 Environmental Stabilization  
- extinguish open flames  
- reduce vibration sources  
- close shutters to control light  
- allow machines to reach ambient temperature  

## 3.3 Master Disc Handling  
- retrieve Master Disc from sealed storage  
- inspect container for moisture or pests  
- allow disc to acclimate for 10–15 minutes  
- **do not** touch the disc with bare hands  

---

# 4. The Ritual Sequence

Calibration Day proceeds in **five phases**, always in the same order.

---

## Phase I — Playback Device Verification

### 4.1.1 Platter Speed Check  
Using the Master Disc’s 440 Hz tone:

- adjust governor until pitch matches reference  
- verify using strobe markings  
- log any deviation in the Speed Ledger  

### 4.1.2 Bearing & Lubrication Check  
- inspect bearings for dryness  
- apply approved lubricants (graphite, plant oils, tallow blends)  
- spin platter by hand to feel for roughness  

### 4.1.3 Tonearm & Tracking Check  
- verify counterweight position  
- check lateral freedom  
- inspect stylus mount for looseness  

---

## Phase II — Stylus Inspection

### 4.2.1 Playback Stylus  
Inspect under magnification:

- no flat spots  
- no chips  
- no asymmetry  
- no corrosion  

Reject stylus if:

- it squeals  
- it produces excessive debris  
- it fails the reference groove test  

### 4.2.2 Cutting Stylus  
- inspect sapphire/ruby tip  
- verify polish  
- check heating element (if present)  
- ensure mount rigidity  

---

## Phase III — Tonal & Mechanical Calibration

### 4.3.1 Frequency Sweep Playback  
Play Band II of the Master Disc:

- listen for resonance peaks  
- note dips or muffling  
- compare across machines  

### 4.3.2 Steady Tone Calibration  
Play Band III:

- verify tonal balance  
- check for flutter  
- detect governor instability  

### 4.3.3 Spoken Phrase Verification  
Play Band IV:

- check consonant clarity  
- detect groove wear  
- evaluate intelligibility  

Apprentices must repeat the phrases aloud to train their ear.

---

## Phase IV — Cross‑Machine Comparison

All machines are played in sequence using the same Master Disc.

Operators listen for:

- tonal differences  
- speed drift  
- mechanical noise  
- stylus behavior  
- horn coloration  

Any machine that deviates significantly is flagged for maintenance.

---

## Phase V — Shared Listening Session

The ritual concludes with a communal listening session.

Purpose:

- reinforce shared tonal memory  
- train apprentices in subjective evaluation  
- maintain cultural continuity  
- celebrate the craft  

Music, announcements, or archival recordings may be played.

---

# 5. Logging & Documentation

Each Calibration Day must produce:

- Speed Ledger entries  
- Stylus Inspection Log  
- Machine Drift Report  
- Resin Condition Notes  
- Apprentice Participation Record  

These logs allow long‑term drift tracking and inter‑settlement comparison.

---

# 6. Failure Conditions & Remediation

If any of the following occur:

- Master Disc shows wear  
- stylus damages a groove  
- machine cannot hold speed  
- tonal balance deviates beyond tolerance  
- resonance peaks appear suddenly  

Then:

1. halt all public playback  
2. perform emergency maintenance  
3. duplicate critical records  
4. notify the Guild Hall if Master Disc is compromised  

---

# 7. Expected Outcomes

- stabilized speed regulation  
- consistent stylus geometry  
- reduced distortion  
- predictable tonal balance  
- early detection of mechanical drift  
- improved operator skill  
- shared cultural memory  
- long‑term compatibility across settlements  

---

# 8. Conclusion

Calibration Day is not merely maintenance — it is the **ritual that keeps the Guild’s voice coherent**.  
Through shared listening, disciplined inspection, and the authority of the Master Disc, settlements maintain precision in a world where materials drift, tools wear, and memory fades.

The ritual ensures that **every month, the system is reborn in alignment**.
