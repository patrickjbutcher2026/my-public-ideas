# Acoustic Calibration Bridge  
**Audio Guild — Mechanical & Acoustic Division**  
**Version 1.0**

The Acoustic Calibration Bridge is a standardized mechanical device used to verify the performance of horn‑based acoustic recording and playback systems.  
It provides a **non‑electrical**, **material‑agnostic**, and **settlement‑independent** method for checking tonal balance, resonance behavior, and mechanical alignment.

This document defines the construction, use, and maintenance of the Acoustic Calibration Bridge.

---

# 1. Purpose

The Acoustic Calibration Bridge exists to:

- calibrate horn geometry  
- detect resonance peaks  
- verify diaphragm tension  
- check cutter head alignment  
- compare acoustic systems across settlements  
- provide fallback calibration when electrical systems fail  

It is the **mechanical counterpart** to the Master Disc.

---

# 2. Concept Overview

The Bridge is a **tunable resonant bar** that produces predictable tones when excited.  
It is placed at the horn throat or recording diaphragm to:

- excite the system  
- reveal resonances  
- expose mechanical drift  
- provide a stable reference tone  

Because it is purely mechanical, it works even when:

- coils fail  
- capacitors drift  
- power is unavailable  
- cutting heads degrade  

---

# 3. Physical Construction

## 3.1 Materials  
The Bridge must be made from:

- **seasoned hardwood** (maple, ash, or oak)  
- or **bronze** (preferred for long‑term stability)  

## 3.2 Dimensions  
Standard dimensions:

- Length: **200–250 mm**  
- Width: **20–30 mm**  
- Thickness: **5–8 mm**  

## 3.3 Tuning Notches  
Three notches are carved or machined into the bar:

- **Low notch** → ~250 Hz  
- **Mid notch** → ~1,000 Hz  
- **High notch** → ~3,000 Hz  

Exact frequencies vary slightly by material but remain stable over time.

## 3.4 Mounting Points  
Two small holes or grooves allow the Bridge to be:

- suspended  
- clamped  
- or held at nodal points  

This prevents damping during use.

# 4. Using the Acoustic Calibration Bridge

The Bridge is used in **three modes**:

1. **Horn Excitation Mode**  
2. **Diaphragm Response Mode**  
3. **Cutter Head Alignment Mode**

---

## 4.1 Horn Excitation Mode

### Procedure  
1. Hold the Bridge at its nodal points (¼ and ¾ length).  
2. Strike gently with a wooden mallet or fingernail.  
3. Position the vibrating Bridge at the horn throat.  
4. Listen for:  
   - resonance peaks  
   - muffling  
   - rattles  
   - throat turbulence  
   - horn coloration  

### Interpretation  
- **Strong peak at low notch** → horn too long or throat too narrow  
- **Weak mid response** → diaphragm tension drift  
- **Harsh high response** → horn flare mismatch  

---

## 4.2 Diaphragm Response Mode

### Procedure  
1. Hold Bridge near the recording diaphragm.  
2. Excite the Bridge using the high or mid notch.  
3. Observe diaphragm movement under angled light.  
4. Listen for:  
   - buzzing  
   - rattling  
   - uneven motion  
   - delayed settling  

### Interpretation  
- **Buzzing** → loose diaphragm screws  
- **Uneven motion** → warped diaphragm  
- **Slow settling** → excessive damping  

---

## 4.3 Cutter Head Alignment Mode

### Procedure  
1. Excite the Bridge using the mid notch.  
2. Hold it near the cutter head.  
3. Observe stylus movement.  
4. Compare stylus motion to expected amplitude.

### Interpretation  
- **Stylus moves diagonally** → misalignment  
- **Stylus barely moves** → hardened damping pad  
- **Stylus over‑responds** → loose suspension  

---

# 5. Calibration Sequence (Standard)

The Bridge is used in this order:

1. Horn Excitation  
2. Diaphragm Response  
3. Cutter Head Alignment  
4. Verification using spoken phrases  
5. Optional: compare with electrical sweep (if available)

This sequence ensures mechanical issues are corrected before tonal evaluation.

---

# 6. Logging & Drift Tracking

Each use of the Bridge must be logged:

- date  
- operator  
- horn model  
- diaphragm material  
- cutter head type  
- observed resonances  
- corrective actions  

Over time, this reveals:

- seasonal drift  
- material fatigue  
- horn throat wear  
- diaphragm aging  

---

# 7. Maintenance of the Bridge

## 7.1 Wood Bridges  
- store in dry environment  
- oil lightly once per year  
- avoid warping  
- re‑cut notches if frequencies drift  

## 7.2 Bronze Bridges  
- wipe with oil to prevent oxidation  
- check for dents  
- re‑tune notches only with Guild approval  

## 7.3 Replacement  
Replace Bridge if:

- cracks appear  
- notches deform  
- frequencies drift beyond tolerance  
- bar becomes warped

# 8. Expected Outcomes

Using the Acoustic Calibration Bridge ensures:

- stable horn performance  
- predictable diaphragm behavior  
- consistent cutter head alignment  
- reduced distortion  
- improved intelligibility  
- reliable acoustic fallback systems  
- long‑term inter‑settlement compatibility

# 9. Conclusion

The Acoustic Calibration Bridge is a simple, durable, and essential tool for maintaining mechanical audio systems in a world where precision must be re‑established regularly.  
It ensures that even without electricity, the Guild’s voice remains clear, stable, and true.

A settlement with a Bridge can always recalibrate its sound.
