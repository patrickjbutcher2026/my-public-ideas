# Shellac Batch‑Alpha Formula  
**Materials Division — Audio Guild Standard**

Batch‑Alpha is the baseline shellac mixture used for all Guild‑approved record production.  
It is optimized for **intelligibility**, **durability**, and **predictable wear**, not high fidelity.  
This formula is manufacturable in any settlement with basic heating, sieving, and mixing capability.

---

# 1. Purpose of Batch‑Alpha

Batch‑Alpha provides:

- stable groove geometry  
- predictable stylus wear  
- low surface noise relative to village‑scale materials  
- long‑term inter‑settlement compatibility  
- resistance to cracking and warping  
- consistent behavior across temperature and humidity changes  

It is the **default** formula for civic announcements, music, televisor soundtracks, and archival recordings.

---

# 2. Ingredient List (By Mass)

| Ingredient | Proportion | Function |
|-----------|------------|----------|
| **Refined Flake Shellac** | 25% | Binder, surface hardness |
| **Fine Slate Flour (Pass‑200 mesh)** | 60% | Structural body, groove stability |
| **Carbon Black (sieved lamp soot)** | 10% | Lubrication, static reduction, opacity |
| **Cotton Flock / Natural Fiber** | 5% | Tensile strength, crack resistance |

---

# 3. Ingredient Requirements

## 3.1 Shellac  
- Must be flake‑refined.  
- Free of bark, insect fragments, and moisture.  
- Stored in sealed clay jars.

## 3.2 Slate Flour  
- Must pass a **200‑mesh sieve**.  
- Coarse particles cause groove tearing and stylus chatter.  
- Should be light gray to dark gray; avoid reddish or sandy impurities.

## 3.3 Carbon Black  
- Must be sieved to remove clumps.  
- Lampblack is acceptable if soot is fine and dry.  
- Provides lubrication and reduces static crackle.

## 3.4 Fibers  
- Cotton, flax, or other natural fibers.  
- Must be short (2–4 mm).  
- Must be clean and pest‑free.

---

# 4. Mixing Protocol (Mandatory)

## 4.1 Heating  
- Warm shellac gently until pliable.  
- Avoid scorching or bubbling.  
- Maintain steady heat using charcoal, alcohol lamp, or kiln‑adjacent shelf.

## 4.2 Incorporation Order  
Add ingredients in this exact sequence:

1. **Slate flour** — added slowly while stirring.  
2. **Carbon black** — sifted through a fine sieve.  
3. **Fibers** — added last to avoid clumping.

## 4.3 Mixing Duration  
- Mix for **12–15 minutes** at stable temperature.  
- Stir continuously and rhythmically.  
- Avoid introducing air.

## 4.4 Degassing  
- Allow mixture to rest until bubbles collapse.  
- Tap container gently to release trapped air.  
- Proper degassing prevents micro‑voids in groove walls.

---

# 5. Quality Diagnostics

## 5.1 Visual Inspection  
A correct batch should be:

- uniformly black  
- matte, not glossy  
- free of streaks or clumps  
- smooth when cooled and pressed between fingers

## 5.2 Bend Test  
A cooled sample should:

- flex slightly before cracking  
- break cleanly  
- show no white stress lines  

## 5.3 Groove Test  
Cut a shallow test groove using a reference stylus:

- walls should be smooth  
- no tearing or chatter  
- stylus should not squeal  
- debris should be fine and powdery  

---

# 6. Stylus Compatibility

To prevent abrasive wear:

- playback stylus must be **softer** than the filler (slate flour).  
- standard playback stylus: **tempered steel**  
- sapphire/ruby allowed **only for cutting**, never playback.

---

# 7. Resin Lot Logging

Each batch must be logged with:

- date  
- resin source  
- slate flour source  
- carbon black source  
- fiber source  
- operator initials  
- mixing duration  
- temperature notes  

This enables long‑term drift tracking and inter‑settlement compatibility.

---

# 8. Storage & Handling

- Store raw shellac in sealed clay jars.  
- Keep carbon black dry and sifted.  
- Protect slate flour from moisture.  
- Keep fibers clean and pest‑free.  
- Store finished records vertically in cool, shaded spaces.
