# Archivist Handbook  
**Audio Guild — Memory & Preservation Division**  
**Version 1.0**

This handbook defines the responsibilities, preservation practices, and long‑term stewardship duties of the Archivist.  
Archivists are the guardians of recorded memory: the voices, stories, histories, and knowledge that define a settlement’s identity.

An Archivist ensures that every recording — from the oldest oral history to the newest civic announcement — remains intelligible for generations.

---

# 1. Role of the Archivist

Archivists are responsible for:

- cataloging and storing all recorded materials  
- maintaining stable environmental conditions  
- inspecting discs for degradation  
- performing safe archival playback  
- duplicating at‑risk recordings  
- maintaining the Archive Ledger  
- training apprentices in preservation practices  
- coordinating with Recordists and Materials Stewards  
- safeguarding the Master Disc and lineage discs  

The Archivist is the settlement’s memory keeper.

---

# 2. Core Competencies

An Archivist must demonstrate mastery in:

## 2.1 Preservation Science  
- understanding shellac aging  
- identifying mold, bloom, and warping  
- predicting long‑term drift  
- stabilizing at‑risk discs  

## 2.2 Cataloging & Documentation  
- maintaining Archive Ledger  
- tracking disc lineage  
- recording playback history  
- documenting degradation patterns  

## 2.3 Safe Playback  
- selecting correct stylus  
- detecting distortion early  
- stopping playback at first sign of damage  
- logging playback events  

## 2.4 Duplication & Restoration  
- preparing blanks for duplication  
- coordinating with Journeymen for cutting  
- verifying duplicate accuracy  

## 2.5 Environmental Control  
- managing humidity  
- controlling temperature  
- preventing pest intrusion  

---

# 3. Daily Responsibilities

Archivists must:

- inspect archive room conditions  
- check for moisture or pests  
- verify disc shelving stability  
- inspect at‑risk discs  
- update Archive Ledger  
- clean playback stylus for archival use  

Daily vigilance prevents irreversible loss.

---

# 4. Weekly Responsibilities

## 4.1 Disc Inspection  
Check for:

- warping  
- surface bloom  
- mold intrusion  
- cracks  
- groove whitening  
- delamination  

## 4.2 Environmental Logging  
Record:

- temperature  
- humidity  
- light exposure  
- unusual events  

## 4.3 Playback Verification  
- test playback on reference discs  
- verify stylus condition  
- check tonal balance  

---

# 5. Monthly Responsibilities — Calibration Day

Archivists support Calibration Day by:

- retrieving archival reference discs  
- verifying safe playback stylus  
- logging playback events  
- evaluating tonal drift in archival material  
- advising on long‑term preservation issues  

Calibration Day ensures that archival playback remains safe and consistent.

---

# 6. Archival Storage Standards

## 6.1 Shelving  
- store discs vertically  
- avoid leaning  
- use wooden or metal shelves  
- ensure shelves are level  

## 6.2 Containers  
- use sealed wooden or clay containers  
- avoid cloth bags for long‑term storage  
- label containers clearly  

## 6.3 Environmental Conditions  
- temperature: **15–22°C**  
- humidity: **30–50%**  
- avoid direct sunlight  
- avoid vibration sources  

## 6.4 Pest Control  
- inspect weekly  
- use sealed containers  
- avoid organic packing materials  

---

# 7. Safe Playback Protocol

## 7.1 Stylus Requirements  
- use only approved archival stylus  
- inspect under magnification  
- reject stylus with any wear  

## 7.2 Pre‑Playback Inspection  
- check disc for cracks  
- check for mold  
- check for warping  
- clean surface gently  

## 7.3 During Playback  
Stop immediately if:

- distortion appears  
- stylus squeals  
- debris increases  
- groove tearing is visible  

## 7.4 Post‑Playback  
- log playback event  
- inspect stylus  
- inspect disc for new damage  

---

# 8. Duplication Protocol

Archivists coordinate duplication of at‑risk discs.

## 8.1 Pre‑Duplication  
- inspect original disc  
- prepare shellac blank  
- verify cutting stylus  
- brief Journeyman or Master  

## 8.2 During Duplication  
- monitor playback  
- monitor cutting  
- log groove behavior  

## 8.3 Post‑Duplication  
- inspect duplicate  
- compare tonal balance  
- verify intelligibility  
- store duplicate separately  

Duplicates must be clearly labeled as such.

---

# 9. Degradation Types & Remedies

| Degradation Type | Cause | Remedy |
|------------------|-------|--------|
| Surface bloom | moisture | dry disc, store properly |
| Warping | heat | flatten gently under weight |
| Groove whitening | stylus wear | duplicate immediately |
| Mold | humidity | clean, isolate, duplicate |
| Cracks | impact | stabilize, duplicate |
| Delamination | resin drift | duplicate urgently |

---

# 10. Archive Ledger Requirements

The Archive Ledger must include:

- disc title  
- recording date  
- cutter identity  
- material batch  
- playback history  
- degradation notes  
- duplication lineage  
- storage location  

The Ledger is the memory of the memory.

---

# 11. Training Apprentices

Archivists must:

- teach safe playback  
- demonstrate disc inspection  
- explain degradation patterns  
- train in environmental control  
- enforce documentation discipline  

Apprentices learn preservation through careful observation.

---

# 12. Collaboration with Other Guild Roles

Archivists work closely with:

- Recordists (duplication sessions)  
- Materials Stewards (resin drift analysis)  
- Machine Tenders (safe playback conditions)  
- Masters (archival evaluations)  

Preservation is a collaborative craft.

The Archivist is the guardian of the Guild’s recorded memory.  
Through careful inspection, disciplined documentation, and safe playback practices, Archivists ensure that the voices of the past remain clear for the future.

The Guild remembers because the Archivist remembers.
