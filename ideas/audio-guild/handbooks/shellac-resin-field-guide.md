# Shellac Resin Field Guide  
**Audio Guild — Materials Science & Preservation Division**  
**Version 1.0**

This field guide provides practical, settlement‑scale instructions for identifying, preparing, evaluating, and troubleshooting shellac‑based resins used in Guild‑approved recording blanks.  
It is designed for use in workshops, field stations, and traveling Guild teams.

Shellac resin is the foundation of every record.  
Understanding its behavior is essential for clarity, durability, and inter‑settlement compatibility.

---

# 1. Purpose of the Field Guide

This guide teaches operators to:

- identify shellac quality  
- evaluate resin drift  
- prepare stable mixtures  
- diagnose cutting and playback issues caused by resin  
- adjust formulas for climate and season  
- maintain consistent Guild standards across settlements  

Shellac is alive — it changes with heat, humidity, age, and handling.  
This guide helps you understand its language.

---

# 2. Components of Guild‑Approved Resin

Guild resin mixtures consist of four primary components:

1. **Shellac (binder)**  
2. **Slate flour (filler)**  
3. **Carbon black (pigment + lubricant)**  
4. **Fibers (reinforcement)**  

Each component affects groove behavior, stylus wear, and tonal clarity.

---

# 3. Shellac Identification & Quality

## 3.1 Visual Inspection  
High‑quality shellac should be:

- translucent amber or deep brown  
- free of white bloom  
- free of insect fragments  
- free of grit  

## 3.2 Smell  
Fresh shellac has:

- a warm, resinous scent  
- no sour or moldy odor  

## 3.3 Break Test  
Bend a thin flake:

- **clean snap** → good  
- **crumbles** → too dry or old  
- **bends without breaking** → too soft or moist  

## 3.4 Heat Behavior  
Warm a small piece:

- should soften evenly  
- should not bubble  
- should not scorch  

---

# 4. Slate Flour

Slate flour provides hardness and groove stability.

## 4.1 Requirements  
- fine powder (200‑mesh or finer)  
- uniform color  
- no visible grains  

## 4.2 Drift Indicators  
- coarse texture → causes groove tearing  
- moisture → causes clumping  
- mineral variation → causes tonal imbalance  

---

# 5. Carbon Black

Carbon black provides:

- lubrication  
- pigment  
- improved debris behavior  

## 5.1 Quality Indicators  
- fine, even powder  
- deep black color  
- no oily residue  

## 5.2 Drift Indicators  
- clumps → moisture intrusion  
- gray color → contamination  
- gritty feel → incomplete combustion  

---

# 6. Fibers

Fibers reinforce the resin and reduce cracking.

## 6.1 Approved Fibers  
- cotton  
- linen  
- plant fibers (processed)  

## 6.2 Requirements  
- length: **2–4 mm**  
- clean  
- dry  

## 6.3 Drift Indicators  
- rot  
- insect damage  
- uneven length  

---

# 7. Standard Resin Formula (Batch‑Alpha)

Batch‑Alpha is the Guild’s baseline resin mixture.

## 7.1 Ratios (by weight)  
- **Shellac: 60%**  
- **Slate flour: 25%**  
- **Carbon black: 10%**  
- **Fibers: 5%**  

## 7.2 Seasonal Adjustments  
- cold climates → increase shellac by 5%  
- hot climates → increase slate flour by 5%  
- humid climates → reduce fibers slightly  

---

# 8. Resin Preparation Procedure

## 8.1 Heating  
- warm shellac slowly  
- avoid direct flame  
- maintain even temperature  

## 8.2 Mixing  
Add ingredients in this order:

1. shellac  
2. slate flour  
3. carbon black  
4. fibers  

Stir continuously for 12–15 minutes.

## 8.3 Degassing  
- allow mixture to rest  
- tap container to release bubbles  

## 8.4 Cooling  
- cool slowly  
- avoid drafts  
- store vertically  

---

# 9. Resin Drift: Identification & Correction

Resin drift is inevitable.  
The goal is early detection and correction.

## 9.1 Brittleness  
**Symptoms:**  
- groove tearing  
- sharp debris  
- cracking blanks  

**Causes:**  
- too much slate flour  
- old shellac  
- cold climate  

**Correction:**  
- increase shellac  
- warm resin before use  

---

## 9.2 Softness  
**Symptoms:**  
- muffled playback  
- stylus drag  
- excessive debris  

**Causes:**  
- too much shellac  
- high humidity  

**Correction:**  
- increase slate flour  
- dry resin before use  

---

## 9.3 Uneven Texture  
**Symptoms:**  
- inconsistent groove depth  
- tonal imbalance  
- debris clumping  

**Causes:**  
- poorly sieved slate flour  
- contaminated carbon black  

**Correction:**  
- re‑sieve materials  
- discard contaminated pigment  

---

## 9.4 Fiber Imbalance  
**Symptoms:**  
- dull consonants  
- tearing at groove edges  
- inconsistent debris  

**Causes:**  
- fiber length variation  
- moisture in fibers  

**Correction:**  
- re‑cut fibers  
- dry fibers thoroughly  

---

# 10. Groove Test Procedure

A groove test reveals resin behavior before cutting a full disc.

## 10.1 Steps  
1. warm a small resin sample  
2. press into a test blank  
3. cut a short groove  
4. inspect debris  
5. inspect groove walls  
6. play back with test stylus  

## 10.2 Interpretation  
- **smooth debris** → good resin  
- **sharp flakes** → too brittle  
- **sticky debris** → too soft  
- **white edges** → fiber imbalance  

---

# 11. Field Troubleshooting Guide

| Symptom | Likely Cause | Correction |
|--------|--------------|-----------|
| Groove tearing | brittle resin | increase shellac |
| Muffled sound | soft resin | increase slate flour |
| Excess debris | soft resin | adjust mixture |
| Stylus drag | moisture | dry resin |
| Cracking blanks | old shellac | replace batch |
| Uneven tone | filler variation | re‑sieve flour |

---

# 12. Storage & Handling

## 12.1 Storage Conditions  
- cool  
- dry  
- sealed containers  
- away from pests  

## 12.2 Shelf Life  
- shellac: **1–3 years**  
- slate flour: **indefinite**  
- carbon black: **indefinite**  
- fibers: **1–2 years**  

## 12.3 Labeling  
Each batch must include:

- date  
- formula  
- operator  
- climate notes  
- drift observations
