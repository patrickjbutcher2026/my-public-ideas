# Recordist’s Guild Handbook — Apprentice Edition  
**Audio Guild — Training & Standards Division**  
**Version 1.0**

This handbook provides the foundational knowledge, practices, and responsibilities required of an Apprentice Recordist.  
It is designed to prepare apprentices for safe, consistent, and culturally responsible operation of Guild‑approved audio systems.

The Apprentice Recordist is the steward of clarity, memory, and continuity.  
This handbook teaches the skills needed to uphold that responsibility.

---

# 1. Role of the Apprentice Recordist

Apprentices are responsible for:

- learning the fundamentals of recording and playback  
- maintaining equipment under supervision  
- assisting in Calibration Day rituals  
- preparing shellac blanks  
- inspecting styli  
- monitoring cutting sessions  
- logging drift and maintenance  
- safeguarding the Master Disc  

Apprentices do not cut Master Discs, but they must understand every step of the process.

---

# 2. Core Principles of the Guild

Every apprentice must internalize the following principles:

## 2.1 Failure Is Expected  
All materials drift.  
All machines wear.  
All precision must be re‑established regularly.

## 2.2 Operators Are Part of the Control Loop  
Your ears, hands, and judgment stabilize the system.

## 2.3 Consumables Are a Design Layer  
Styli, wax masters, and shellac blanks are sacrificial.  
Their wear protects the system.

## 2.4 Village‑Scale Manufacturability  
All improvements must be achievable with simple tools and local materials.

## 2.5 Cultural Continuity  
Your work preserves voices, stories, and knowledge across generations.

---

# 3. Daily Responsibilities

Apprentices must perform the following tasks each day:

## 3.1 Equipment Inspection  
- dust playback devices  
- check tonearm freedom  
- inspect stylus for wear  
- verify platter cleanliness  
- check horn throat for debris  

## 3.2 Workspace Preparation  
- sweep floors  
- organize tools  
- warm shellac samples for resin checks  
- prepare magnifying lenses  

## 3.3 Logbook Maintenance  
- record observations  
- note drift or unusual behavior  
- track stylus usage time  

---

# 4. Weekly Responsibilities

## 4.1 Lubrication  
- apply approved lubricants to bearings  
- clean excess oil  
- check for contamination  

## 4.2 Horn Maintenance  
- clean throat  
- inspect diaphragm tension  
- check for cracks or leaks  

## 4.3 Resin Diagnostics  
Assist Materials Stewards with:

- visual inspection  
- bend tests  
- groove tests  
- debris analysis  

---

# 5. Monthly Responsibilities — Calibration Day

Apprentices play a central role in Calibration Day:

- prepare workspace  
- retrieve Master Disc  
- inspect playback styli  
- assist with speed calibration  
- observe tonal verification  
- repeat spoken calibration phrases  
- log results  

Calibration Day is the most important training event of the month.

---

# 6. Shellac Blank Preparation

Apprentices must learn to prepare blanks for cutting.

## 6.1 Heating  
- warm shellac evenly  
- avoid scorching  
- maintain steady temperature  

## 6.2 Pressing  
- press mixture into metal‑backed discs  
- ensure uniform thickness  
- avoid trapped air  

## 6.3 Cooling  
- cool slowly to prevent warping  
- store vertically  

## 6.4 Inspection  
- check for bubbles  
- check for cracks  
- check for uneven surfaces  

---

# 7. Stylus Handling & Inspection

## 7.1 Handling Rules  
- never touch tip with bare fingers  
- store in waxed cloth  
- keep cutting styli separate  
- avoid moisture  

## 7.2 Inspection Procedure  
Use magnification to check for:

- flat spots  
- chips  
- asymmetry  
- corrosion  

## 7.3 Replacement  
Apprentices may replace playback styli under supervision.

---

# 8. Cutting Session Duties

During cutting sessions, apprentices must:

- monitor stylus sound  
- watch debris behavior  
- adjust stylus heater (if present)  
- log groove pitch settings  
- inspect grooves after cutting  
- assist with carriage movement  

Apprentices do not operate the cutting head independently until certified.

---

# 9. Playback Session Duties

Apprentices must:

- cue records safely  
- monitor tonal balance  
- detect distortion  
- adjust tracking force  
- clean stylus between sides  
- log playback issues  

Playback is a diagnostic tool as much as a listening activity.

---

# 10. Drift Detection Skills

Apprentices must learn to detect:

- pitch wobble  
- flutter  
- tonal imbalance  
- resonance peaks  
- muffled consonants  
- excessive hiss  
- groove tearing  

These skills are developed through repeated listening and comparison.

---

# 11. Safety Protocols

## 11.1 Mechanical Safety  
- keep hands clear of moving parts  
- secure loose clothing  
- avoid leaning over platter  

## 11.2 Chemical Safety  
- handle hot shellac with tools  
- avoid inhaling carbon black  
- store resins properly  

## 11.3 Archival Safety  
- never touch Master Disc surface  
- use only approved playback stylus  
- stop immediately if distortion appears  

---

# 12. Path to Journeyman Status

To advance, apprentices must demonstrate:

- consistent equipment maintenance  
- accurate drift logging  
- safe stylus handling  
- competent shellac blank preparation  
- reliable tonal judgment  
- successful participation in Calibration Day  
- ability to assist in cutting sessions  

A Guild Master evaluates readiness.

---

# 13. Conclusion

The Apprentice Recordist is the foundation of the Guild’s future.  
By mastering these skills and responsibilities, you ensure that voices, stories, and knowledge remain clear and alive across generations.

Your work preserves the memory of the world.
