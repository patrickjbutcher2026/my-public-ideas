# Recordist’s Guild Handbook — Journeyman Edition  
**Audio Guild — Training & Standards Division**  
**Version 1.0**

This handbook defines the responsibilities, competencies, and advanced practices expected of a Journeyman Recordist.  
Journeymen are the operational backbone of the Guild: they maintain machines, cut program discs, train apprentices, and uphold the standards that preserve clarity and cultural continuity across settlements.

A Journeyman is not yet a Master — but they are trusted with the Guild’s voice.

---

# 1. Role of the Journeyman Recordist

Journeymen are responsible for:

- operating cutting lathes for program discs  
- maintaining playback and cutting equipment  
- diagnosing drift and mechanical faults  
- training and supervising apprentices  
- performing Calibration Day procedures  
- preparing and evaluating shellac blanks  
- managing stylus inventories  
- safeguarding archival materials  
- ensuring inter‑settlement compatibility  

Journeymen may cut **program discs** but not **Master Discs**.

---

# 2. Core Competencies

A Journeyman must demonstrate mastery in:

## 2.1 Tonal Judgment  
- identifying resonance peaks  
- detecting flutter and wow  
- recognizing stylus wear by sound alone  
- evaluating horn coloration  
- assessing groove quality  

## 2.2 Mechanical Skill  
- adjusting governors  
- aligning cutter heads  
- tensioning diaphragms  
- maintaining bearings  
- repairing tonearms  

## 2.3 Material Knowledge  
- diagnosing resin drift  
- evaluating shellac batches  
- identifying contamination  
- adjusting formulas within tolerance  

## 2.4 Instruction & Leadership  
- training apprentices  
- enforcing safety protocols  
- maintaining logs  
- leading Calibration Day rituals  

---

# 3. Daily Responsibilities

Journeymen must:

- inspect all machines before use  
- verify stylus condition  
- check platter speed stability  
- clean horn throats  
- maintain workshop order  
- review apprentice logs  
- prepare cutting sessions  

Journeymen are the first line of defense against drift.

---

# 4. Weekly Responsibilities

## 4.1 Equipment Maintenance  
- lubricate bearings  
- clean and re‑seat diaphragms  
- inspect governor springs  
- check leadscrew backlash  
- polish stylus mounts  

## 4.2 Resin & Material Checks  
- evaluate new resin batches  
- perform groove tests  
- log drift patterns  
- adjust formulas if needed  

## 4.3 Apprentice Oversight  
- review apprentice work  
- correct unsafe practices  
- assign training tasks  

---

# 5. Monthly Responsibilities — Calibration Day

Journeymen lead Calibration Day.

## 5.1 Preparation  
- prepare workspace  
- retrieve Master Disc  
- inspect all playback styli  
- warm shellac samples  
- set up Acoustic Calibration Bridge  

## 5.2 Execution  
- calibrate platter speeds  
- evaluate tonal balance  
- compare machines  
- instruct apprentices in listening skills  
- log all deviations  

## 5.3 Post‑Ritual Duties  
- schedule repairs  
- replace worn components  
- update Drift Reports  

Calibration Day is the Journeyman’s proving ground.

---

# 6. Cutting Program Discs

Journeymen are authorized to cut:

- civic announcements  
- music recordings  
- televisor soundtracks  
- archival duplicates (non‑Master)  

## 6.1 Pre‑Cut Checklist  
- stylus inspected  
- platter speed verified  
- shellac blank warmed  
- groove pitch set  
- cutting head aligned  
- horn or electrical head tested  

## 6.2 During Cutting  
- monitor stylus sound  
- watch debris behavior  
- adjust heater (if present)  
- maintain steady carriage motion  
- log groove pitch and depth  

## 6.3 Post‑Cut Inspection  
- inspect groove under magnification  
- check for chatter or tearing  
- verify depth consistency  
- cool blank slowly  

---

# 7. Advanced Drift Detection

Journeymen must detect subtle forms of drift:

## 7.1 Mechanical Drift  
- governor lag  
- bearing rumble  
- tonearm resonance  
- diaphragm fatigue  
- horn throat turbulence  

## 7.2 Material Drift  
- resin brittleness  
- carbon black clumping  
- slate flour coarseness  
- fiber distribution issues  

## 7.3 Acoustic Drift  
- tonal imbalance  
- muffled consonants  
- harsh treble  
- weak midrange  

Journeymen must log all drift patterns and propose corrective actions.

---

# 8. Safety Protocols

## 8.1 Mechanical Safety  
- secure loose clothing  
- avoid leaning over moving platters  
- keep tools organized  
- stop machines before adjustments  

## 8.2 Chemical Safety  
- handle hot shellac with tools  
- avoid inhaling carbon black  
- store resins in sealed containers  

## 8.3 Archival Safety  
- never use unverified stylus on archival discs  
- stop playback immediately if distortion appears  
- store Master Disc in sealed container  

---

# 9. Training Apprentices

Journeymen must:

- teach fundamentals of playback and cutting  
- demonstrate safe stylus handling  
- guide resin diagnostics  
- lead listening exercises  
- correct errors without discouraging learning  
- model calm, precise behavior  

A Journeyman’s apprentices reflect their discipline.

---

# 10. Path to Mastery

To advance to Master Recordist, a Journeyman must:

- demonstrate flawless Calibration Day leadership  
- cut program discs with consistent quality  
- diagnose and repair complex mechanical faults  
- train apprentices to competence  
- maintain perfect archival safety record  
- pass evaluation by a Guild Master  

Mastery is earned through consistency, clarity, and judgment.

---

# 11. Conclusion

The Journeyman Recordist is the Guild’s working expert — a technician, teacher, and guardian of clarity.  
Through disciplined practice and careful listening, Journeymen preserve the voices of their settlements and ensure that knowledge endures across generations.

Your work shapes the sound of the world.
