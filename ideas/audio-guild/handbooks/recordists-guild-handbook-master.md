# Recordist’s Guild Handbook — Master Edition  
**Audio Guild — High Council of Recordists**  
**Version 1.0**

This handbook defines the responsibilities, authorities, and advanced practices expected of a Master Recordist.  
Masters are the custodians of the Guild’s deepest knowledge, the protectors of its standards, and the stewards of its cultural memory.

A Master Recordist is entrusted with the Guild’s most sacred tasks — including the creation, verification, and protection of Master Discs.

---

# 1. Role of the Master Recordist

Masters are responsible for:

- cutting and validating Master Discs  
- training Journeymen and evaluating apprentices  
- maintaining the highest‑precision equipment  
- diagnosing complex mechanical, acoustic, and material drift  
- overseeing Calibration Day rituals  
- safeguarding archival collections  
- resolving inter‑settlement compatibility issues  
- advising settlement leadership on audio matters  
- maintaining the Guild’s oral and technical traditions  

Masters are the final authority on all matters of sound.

---

# 2. Core Mastery Domains

A Master Recordist must demonstrate excellence in:

## 2.1 Acoustic Judgment  
- identifying subtle resonance patterns  
- detecting micro‑flutter and micro‑wow  
- evaluating horn geometry by ear  
- distinguishing stylus wear stages by sound alone  
- assessing tonal drift across seasons  

## 2.2 Mechanical Expertise  
- rebuilding governors  
- machining replacement bearings  
- re‑tensioning and re‑seating diaphragms  
- aligning cutter heads to sub‑millimeter precision  
- diagnosing vibration paths through chassis structures  

## 2.3 Materials Science  
- refining shellac mixtures  
- identifying resin contamination sources  
- adjusting formulas for climate and season  
- evaluating slate flour granularity  
- predicting long‑term drift in archival discs  

## 2.4 Instruction & Leadership  
- training Journeymen to independence  
- evaluating apprentices for advancement  
- leading Calibration Day with authority  
- maintaining discipline and safety  
- preserving Guild traditions  

---

# 3. Daily Responsibilities

Masters must:

- inspect all cutting and playback machines  
- verify stylus inventories  
- evaluate resin batches  
- review Journeyman and Apprentice logs  
- maintain the Master Disc vault  
- oversee cutting sessions  
- ensure tonal consistency across devices  

Masters set the standard for the entire settlement.

## 4.2 Material Oversight

## 4.3 Instruction  
- conduct advanced listening sessions  
- teach mechanical repair techniques  
- evaluate apprentice progress  

---

# 5. Monthly Responsibilities — Calibration Day Leadership

Masters lead Calibration Day with full authority.

## 5.1 Preparation  
- inspect Master Disc  
- prepare Acoustic Calibration Bridge  
- verify all playback styli  
- ensure environmental stability  
- brief Journeymen on responsibilities  

## 5.2 Execution  
- calibrate all machines  
- evaluate tonal balance across devices  
- detect drift patterns  
- instruct apprentices in advanced listening  
- log all deviations and assign corrective tasks  

## 5.3 Post‑Ritual  
- schedule repairs  
- update Drift Reports  
- evaluate settlement‑wide audio health  

Calibration Day is the Master’s primary ritual duty.

---

# 6. Cutting Master Discs

Only Masters may cut Master Discs.

## 6.1 Pre‑Cut Requirements  
- cutting stylus inspected under high magnification  
- platter speed stabilized to reference tone  
- shellac blank verified for purity  
- cutting head aligned to highest precision  
- horn or electrical head tested for resonance  

## 6.2 During Cutting  
- monitor stylus sound continuously  
- adjust heater temperature  
- maintain steady carriage motion  
- detect micro‑chatter  
- log groove pitch, depth, and drift  

## 6.3 Post‑Cut Verification  
- inspect groove under magnification  
- verify sweep smoothness  
- check tonal accuracy  
- test spoken calibration phrases  
- approve or reject disc  

Rejected Master Discs must be destroyed.

---

# 7. Advanced Drift Detection

Masters must detect drift invisible to others.

## 7.1 Mechanical Drift  
- microscopic bearing wear  
- governor spring fatigue  
- platter mass imbalance  
- tonearm resonance shifts  
- cutter head suspension drift  

## 7.2 Material Drift  
- resin polymerization changes  
- carbon black moisture absorption  
- slate flour mineral variation  
- fiber degradation  

## 7.3 Acoustic Drift  
- horn throat turbulence  
- diaphragm stiffness changes  
- seasonal humidity effects  
- long‑term tonal shifts  

Masters maintain the settlement’s acoustic identity.

---

# 8. Archival Stewardship

Masters are the guardians of the settlement’s recorded memory.

## 8.1 Archival Playback  
- use only approved archival stylus  
- stop immediately if distortion appears  
- log all playback events  

## 8.2 Preservation  
- store discs vertically  
- maintain stable temperature  
- inspect for mold, warping, or bloom  
- duplicate at first sign of degradation  

## 8.3 Vault Management  
- maintain sealed containers  
- track disc lineage  
- protect Master Discs from light, heat, and vibration  

---

# 9. Inter‑Settlement Responsibilities

Masters ensure compatibility across the Guild.

## 9.1 Traveling Evaluations

- evaluate resin batches

## 9.2 Standard Enforcement

## 9.3 Cultural Continuity

- maintain consistent calibration phrases  
- uphold Guild rituals
