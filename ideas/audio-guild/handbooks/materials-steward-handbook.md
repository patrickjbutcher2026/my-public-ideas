# Materials Steward Handbook  
**Audio Guild — Materials Science & Preservation Division**  
**Version 1.0**

This handbook defines the responsibilities, knowledge domains, and operational practices required of a Materials Steward.  
Materials Stewards are the guardians of the Guild’s physical substrate: the resins, fillers, fibers, pigments, and blanks that make recording possible.

A Materials Steward ensures that every record — from civic announcement to Master Disc — begins with stable, predictable, and culturally compatible materials.

---

# 1. Role of the Materials Steward

Materials Stewards are responsible for:

- preparing and refining shellac mixtures  
- evaluating resin quality and drift  
- maintaining the Resin Drift Ledger  
- preparing blanks for cutting  
- sieving and grading slate flour  
- producing carbon black  
- maintaining fiber stocks  
- training apprentices in material handling  
- ensuring inter‑settlement material compatibility  
- supporting Master Disc creation  

The Materials Steward is the foundation of the Guild’s material science.

---

# 2. Core Competencies

A Materials Steward must demonstrate mastery in:

## 2.1 Resin Chemistry  
- understanding shellac behavior  
- identifying contamination  
- adjusting formulas for climate  
- predicting long‑term drift  

## 2.2 Particle Science  
- sieving slate flour  
- identifying coarse contaminants  
- evaluating carbon black fineness  
- understanding filler‑to‑binder ratios  

## 2.3 Mechanical Behavior  
- predicting groove tearing  
- evaluating stylus wear patterns  
- understanding resin hardness and brittleness  

## 2.4 Environmental Sensitivity  
- tracking humidity effects  
- monitoring temperature drift  
- adjusting storage conditions  

## 2.5 Documentation & Logging  
- maintaining Resin Drift Ledger  
- recording batch lineage  
- tracking seasonal variations  

---

# 3. Daily Responsibilities

Materials Stewards must:

- inspect resin stores  
- check for moisture intrusion  
- stir and aerate carbon black containers  
- verify fiber cleanliness  
- prepare small resin samples for testing  
- maintain workshop cleanliness  

Daily vigilance prevents long‑term drift.

---

# 4. Weekly Responsibilities

## 4.1 Resin Diagnostics  
Perform:

- visual inspection  
- bend tests  
- hardness checks  
- groove tests  
- debris analysis  

## 4.2 Slate Flour Maintenance  
- sieve flour through 200‑mesh screen  
- remove coarse particles  
- log flour source and quality  

## 4.3 Carbon Black Production  
- burn lamp oil or plant oils  
- collect soot  
- sieve to remove clumps  
- store in sealed clay jars  

## 4.4 Fiber Preparation  
- cut fibers to 2–4 mm  
- remove dust  
- store in dry containers  

---

# 5. Monthly Responsibilities — Calibration Day Support

Materials Stewards play a critical role in Calibration Day.

## 5.1 Preparation  
- warm resin samples  
- prepare groove test blanks  
- inspect carbon black fineness  
- verify slate flour consistency  

## 5.2 During Ritual  
- assist Journeymen with resin diagnostics  
- evaluate groove debris  
- log material drift  
- advise on tonal anomalies caused by materials  

## 5.3 Post‑Ritual  
- update Resin Drift Ledger  
- adjust formulas for next month  
- prepare new batches if required  

---

# 6. Shellac Batch Preparation

Materials Stewards are responsible for producing Batch‑Alpha and other Guild‑approved mixtures.

## 6.1 Ingredient Order  
1. shellac  
2. slate flour  
3. carbon black  
4. fibers  

## 6.2 Heating  
- warm shellac gently  
- avoid scorching  
- maintain steady temperature  

## 6.3 Mixing  
- stir continuously  
- avoid introducing air  
- mix for 12–15 minutes  

## 6.4 Degassing  
- allow mixture to rest  
- tap container to release bubbles  

## 6.5 Cooling  
- cool slowly  
- avoid drafts  
- store vertically  

---

# 7. Blank Preparation

## 7.1 Pressing  
- press resin onto metal‑backed discs  
- ensure uniform thickness  
- avoid trapped air  

## 7.2 Inspection  
- check for bubbles  
- check for cracks  
- check for uneven surfaces  

## 7.3 Storage  
- store vertically  
- keep in cool, dry environment  
- protect from pests  

---

# 8. Drift Detection & Correction

Materials Stewards must detect and correct drift in:

## 8.1 Resin Behavior  
- brittleness  
- softness  
- surface bloom  
- color shifts  

## 8.2 Slate Flour  
- coarseness  
- mineral variation  
- moisture absorption  

## 8.3 Carbon Black  
- clumping  
- contamination  
- moisture intrusion  

## 8.4 Fibers  
- rot  
- insect damage  
- length variation  

Corrective actions include:

- adjusting shellac ratio  
- re‑sieving flour  
- re‑sieving carbon black  
- drying fibers  
- replacing contaminated stock  

---

# 9. Safety Protocols

## 9.1 Heat Safety  
- handle hot shellac with tools  
- avoid open flames near resin  

## 9.2 Dust Safety  
- avoid inhaling carbon black  
- sieve materials outdoors if possible  

## 9.3 Chemical Safety  
- store resins in sealed containers  
- avoid moisture contamination  

## 9.4 Archival Safety  
- never use untested resin for archival blanks  
- log all batches used for duplication  

---

# 10. Training Apprentices

Materials Stewards must:

- teach resin diagnostics  
- demonstrate proper sieving  
- instruct in blank preparation  
- explain drift patterns  
- enforce safety protocols  

Apprentices learn material science through repetition and observation.

---

# 11. Collaboration with Recordists

Materials Stewards work closely with:

- Journeymen (cutting sessions)  
- Masters (Master Disc creation)  
- Machine Tenders (bearing lubrication compatibility)  
- Archivists (long‑term storage conditions)  

Material science is inseparable from acoustic performance.

The Materials Steward is the Guild’s guardian of physical clarity.
