# Machine Tender Handbook  
**Audio Guild — Mechanical Systems Division**  
**Version 1.0**

This handbook defines the responsibilities, maintenance practices, and diagnostic skills required of a Machine Tender.  
Machine Tenders are the guardians of the Guild’s mechanical infrastructure: the platters, governors, bearings, tonearms, and drive systems that make recording and playback possible.

A Machine Tender ensures that every device runs smoothly, safely, and predictably — the silent foundation beneath every Guild recording.

---

# 1. Role of the Machine Tender

Machine Tenders are responsible for:

- maintaining playback and cutting machines  
- lubricating bearings and drive systems  
- inspecting and adjusting governors  
- aligning tonearms and cutter heads  
- diagnosing mechanical drift  
- repairing worn or damaged components  
- supporting Calibration Day procedures  
- training apprentices in mechanical safety  
- maintaining the Machine Drift Ledger  

Machine Tenders keep the Guild’s machines alive.

---

# 2. Core Competencies

A Machine Tender must demonstrate mastery in:

## 2.1 Mechanical Precision  
- bearing alignment  
- platter balancing  
- governor calibration  
- tonearm geometry  
- leadscrew maintenance  

## 2.2 Vibration Control  
- identifying rumble sources  
- isolating vibration paths  
- reinforcing chassis structures  
- damping resonant components  

## 2.3 Drive System Knowledge  
- spring motor repair  
- falling‑weight drive tuning  
- belt and pulley maintenance  
- torque stability under load  

## 2.4 Safety & Reliability  
- preventing mechanical failures  
- enforcing safe operation  
- maintaining clean, organized workspaces  

---

# 3. Daily Responsibilities

Machine Tenders must:

- inspect all machines before use  
- check platter spin‑down time  
- verify governor freedom of movement  
- inspect tonearm pivots  
- clean dust from mechanical joints  
- listen for rumble or scraping  
- log any anomalies  

Daily inspection prevents catastrophic drift.

---

# 4. Weekly Responsibilities

## 4.1 Lubrication  
- apply approved lubricants to bearings  
- clean excess oil  
- inspect for contamination  
- verify wick or reservoir function  

## 4.2 Drive System Maintenance  
- inspect belts or cords  
- check spring tension  
- verify falling‑weight travel  
- clean pulleys and gears  

## 4.3 Tonearm & Cutter Head  
- check pivot friction  
- inspect counterweights  
- verify stylus mount rigidity  
- test damping pads  

---

# 5. Monthly Responsibilities — Calibration Day

Machine Tenders play a central role in Calibration Day.

## 5.1 Preparation  
- clean all mechanical assemblies  
- inspect governor springs  
- verify platter flatness  
- prepare strobe lamps or slatted sunlight  
- check drive system torque  

## 5.2 During Ritual  
- adjust governors to reference tone  
- compare machines for drift  
- identify mechanical anomalies  
- assist Journeymen and Masters with tonal evaluation  

## 5.3 Post‑Ritual  
- schedule repairs  
- replace worn components  
- update Machine Drift Ledger  

Calibration Day is the Machine Tender’s primary diagnostic event.

---

# 6. Governor Maintenance

The governor is the heart of mechanical stability.

## 6.1 Inspection  
- check ball symmetry  
- inspect arms for bending  
- verify spring tension  
- clean friction collar  

## 6.2 Calibration  
- warm machine  
- play reference tone  
- adjust tension screw  
- verify with strobe markings  

## 6.3 Failure Modes  
- flutter → sticky collar  
- drift → spring fatigue  
- wobble → uneven ball lift  
- rumble → dry bearings  

---

# 7. Bearing & Platter Maintenance

## 7.1 Bearing Care  
- clean spindle  
- inspect sleeve for wear  
- apply fine oil  
- check for lateral play  

## 7.2 Platter Care  
- verify flatness  
- inspect rim for dents  
- clean strobe markings  
- check for mass imbalance  

## 7.3 Spin‑Down Test  
A healthy platter should:

- spin smoothly  
- slow gradually  
- stop without wobble  

---

# 8. Tonearm & Cutter Head Alignment

## 8.1 Tonearm  
- verify pivot freedom  
- check lateral balance  
- inspect counterweight position  
- ensure no binding  

## 8.2 Cutter Head  
- verify vertical alignment  
- inspect suspension  
- check damping block  
- test stylus movement  

## 8.3 Alignment Tools  
- straightedge  
- feeler gauges  
- magnifying lens  
- reference stylus  

---

# 9. Vibration & Resonance Control

Machine Tenders must identify and eliminate vibration sources.

## 9.1 Common Sources  
- loose screws  
- dry bearings  
- warped platters  
- unstable chassis  
- misaligned tonearms  

## 9.2 Remedies  
- tighten fasteners  
- lubricate bearings  
- reinforce chassis  
- add damping pads  
- isolate horn mount  

## 9.3 Resonance Testing  
Use:

- tapping tests  
- Acoustic Calibration Bridge  
- listening for hum or ringing  

---

# 10. Drift Detection & Logging

Machine Tenders maintain the **Machine Drift Ledger**.

## 10.1 Drift Types  
- speed drift  
- mechanical noise  
- governor lag  
- platter wobble  
- tonearm friction  

## 10.2 Logging Requirements  
Record:

- date  
- machine ID  
- observed drift  
- corrective actions  
- operator initials  

Long‑term drift patterns reveal hidden failures.

---

# 11. Safety Protocols

## 11.1 Mechanical Safety  
- keep hands clear of moving parts  
- secure loose clothing  
- stop machines before adjustments  

## 11.2 Tool Safety  
- keep tools sharp and clean  
- store tools properly  
- avoid makeshift repairs  

## 11.3 Workshop Safety  
- maintain clean floors  
- avoid clutter  
- ensure proper lighting  

---

# 12. Training Apprentices

Machine Tenders must:

- teach lubrication techniques  
- demonstrate governor calibration  
- explain vibration paths  
- supervise mechanical repairs  
- enforce safety protocols  

Apprentices learn mechanical intuition through repetition.

---

# 13. Collaboration with Other Guild Roles

Machine Tenders work closely with:

- Recordists (cutting sessions)  
- Materials Stewards (lubricant compatibility)  
- Archivists (safe playback conditions)  
- Masters (complex repairs and evaluations)  

Mechanical stability underpins all Guild operations.

# 14. Conclusion

The Machine Tender is the Guild’s mechanical guardian.  
Through careful maintenance, precise calibration, and vigilant drift detection, Machine Tenders ensure that every machine — from playback device to cutter lathe — operates with stability, clarity, and reliability.

The Guild’s sound depends on the Tender’s hands.
