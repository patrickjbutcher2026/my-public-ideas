# Post‑Industrial Scout Glider Concept
*A low‑energy, ground‑launched reconnaissance aircraft for a post‑industrial society.*

## Purpose
The scout glider provides:

- short‑range reconnaissance  
- message delivery  
- environmental observation  
- shoreline and ice scouting  
- search and rescue  
- inter‑settlement communication  

It is designed for a world with limited fuel, limited industry, and localized communities.

## Operational Concept
The glider is launched using a **ground‑powered flywheel winch**.  
Once airborne, it:

- glides efficiently  
- uses thermals and ridge lift  
- may use a tiny electric sustainer motor  
- lands on short, simple strips  

This minimizes onboard complexity while maximizing range.

## Key Advantages
### No Liquid Fuel
Powered entirely by:

- flywheel energy  
- wind  
- water  
- optional small battery  

### Minimal Industrial Requirements
Airframe built from:

- wood  
- canvas  
- bamboo  
- simple aluminum fittings  
- bicycle‑grade bearings  

### Low Maintenance
No engine overhaul, no fuel system, no electronics.

### High Utility
A single glider can scout 20–80 km, deliver messages, survey coastlines, and support SAR.

## Use Cases
- reconnaissance  
- communication  
- search and rescue  
- mapping and surveying  
- training and cultural value  

## Required Infrastructure
- small airstrip or launch track  
- energy shed with flywheel  
- workshop for repairs  
- trained operators  
- storage hangar  

## Cultural Role
The glider becomes a symbol of:

- community skill  
- shared labor  
- inter‑settlement connection  
- post‑industrial resilience
