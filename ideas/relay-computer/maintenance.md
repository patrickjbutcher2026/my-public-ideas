# Maintenance Doctrine

This document defines the operational upkeep required to keep the relay computer reliable over long periods of use. Maintenance is not an afterthought — it is part of the machine’s architecture. A relay computer is a civic instrument, and its longevity depends on predictable, routine care.

The procedures below assume workshop‑level tools and trained operators.

---

## Philosophy of Maintenance

The relay computer is designed to:

- reveal its own wear  
- fail loudly rather than silently  
- be repaired with simple tools  
- be maintained by trained operators, not specialists  

Maintenance is a **core civic practice**, not a technical burden.

---

## Daily Tasks

These tasks are performed at the start or end of each operating day.

### Visual Inspection
- Check relay banks for dust accumulation  
- Verify no relay armatures are stuck  
- Inspect tape path for debris  
- Confirm plugboard wiring is seated correctly  

### Power Check
- Measure supply voltage (target 12 V)  
- Confirm no excessive ripple or flicker  

### Tape Reader Check
- Ensure sensing pins move freely  
- Verify tape alignment guides are clean  

---

## Weekly Tasks

Performed once per week during normal operation.

### Relay Cleaning
- Wipe relay contacts with a dry, lint‑free card  
- Check for pitting or discoloration  
- Verify consistent armature travel

### Tape Path Cleaning
- Clean sprocket wheel  
- Remove dust from guides and rollers  
- Inspect for wear on tape edges

### ALU Inspection
- Verify gears rotate smoothly  
- Check for binding or uneven resistance  
- Confirm summing elements return to neutral

---

## Monthly Tasks

Performed every 4–5 weeks.

### Lubrication
- Apply minimal oil to gear shafts  
- Lubricate bearings and bushings  
- Avoid over‑lubrication (attracts dust)

### Electrical Checks
- Measure coil resistance of a sample of relays  
- Inspect wiring for fraying or corrosion  
- Tighten terminal screws if needed

### Register Bank Inspection
- Verify latch behavior  
- Check indicator alignment  
- Confirm manual reset levers operate smoothly

---

## Quarterly Tasks

Performed every three months.

### Deep Relay Service
- Remove relay banks  
- Clean contacts with isopropyl alcohol  
- Inspect springs for fatigue  
- Replace any relay showing inconsistent pull‑in behavior

### Tape Reader Calibration
- Verify row spacing alignment  
- Check sensing pin spring tension  
- Inspect sprocket wear

### Plugboard Audit
- Inspect all sockets for looseness  
- Replace worn patch cords  
- Verify routing against documentation

---

## Annual Tasks

Performed once per year or after 1,000 operating hours.

### Full System Calibration
- Run calibration tape  
- Verify timing alignment across all phases  
- Confirm COMMIT boundary behavior  
- Adjust escapement or motor governor if needed

### Structural Inspection
- Check frame alignment  
- Inspect mounting points  
- Verify vibration dampers are intact

### Environmental Review
- Evaluate dust load in operating environment  
- Inspect ventilation  
- Review humidity and temperature logs

---

## Signs of Drift or Wear

Operators should watch for:

- relay chatter  
- inconsistent tape advance  
- ALU stalls or hesitation  
- increased power draw  
- audible irregularities in timing  
- intermittent register updates  

These symptoms indicate maintenance is required before further operation.

---

## Maintenance Records

All maintenance must be logged in the mechanics logbook:

- date  
- operator  
- tasks performed  
- anomalies observed  
- parts replaced  
- calibration results  

The logbook is part of the machine’s institutional memory.

---

## Summary

The relay computer is designed to be:

- maintainable  
- inspectable  
- repairable  
- predictable  

Routine maintenance ensures the machine remains a reliable civic tool for decades. The procedures in this document form the backbone of that reliability.
