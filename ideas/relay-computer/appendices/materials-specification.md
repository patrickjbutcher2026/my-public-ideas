# Materials Specification

This appendix defines the recommended materials for constructing, maintaining, and repairing the relay computer. The goal is not precision engineering but **repeatability**, **durability**, and **workshop‑level accessibility**. All materials listed here are chosen for availability, stability, and compatibility with hand‑tool fabrication.

The machine must be buildable in community workshops, civic labs, and small fabrication spaces.

---

## Structural Materials

### Frame
- Preferred: hardwood (oak, maple, ash)  
- Acceptable: softwood (pine, fir) with reinforcement  
- Alternative: mild steel angle or square tube  

### Panels and Mounting Plates
- Preferred: 3–6 mm plywood or hardwood sheet  
- Acceptable: aluminum sheet (1–2 mm)  
- Avoid: plastics that warp under heat or UV  

### Fasteners
- Wood screws, machine screws, and bolts in standard sizes  
- Brass or stainless steel preferred for corrosion resistance  

---

## Relay Components

### Relays
- Standard 12 V DC mechanical relays  
- Coil resistance tolerance: ±15%  
- Contact material: silver alloy or copper alloy  
- Contact rating: low‑current signal switching  

### Relay Mounting
- Wooden rails with threaded inserts  
- Metal brackets for high‑density banks  

### Contact Cleaning Materials
- Lint‑free cards  
- Isopropyl alcohol (90%+)  
- Non‑abrasive polishing strips  

---

## Wiring and Connectors

### Wire
- 20–24 AWG stranded copper for signal lines  
- 16–18 AWG for power distribution  
- Color‑coded insulation for clarity  

### Insulation
- PVC or silicone  
- Cloth‑wrapped wire acceptable for historical builds  

### Connectors
- Crimp lugs  
- Screw terminals  
- Soldered joints where appropriate  

### Looms
- Cotton or nylon lacing cord  
- Reusable cable ties for serviceable bundles  

---

## Plugboard Materials

### Board
- Phenolic sheet  
- Hardwood panel with embedded sockets  
- Aluminum plate with insulating standoffs  

### Sockets
- Banana jacks  
- 2 mm or 4 mm laboratory sockets  
- Spring‑contact matrix sockets  

### Patch Cords
- Flexible stranded wire  
- Reinforced strain relief  
- Distinct colors for routing clarity  

---

## Tape Reader Materials

### Tape
- Heavy paper (0.1–0.4 mm)  
- Mylar or PET film for long‑life jobs  
- Avoid brittle plastics  

### Sensing Pins
- Hardened steel or brass  
- Spring steel for return springs  

### Sprocket Wheel
- Aluminum or brass  
- Machined or hand‑filed teeth acceptable  

### Guides
- Hardwood or UHMW plastic  
- Low‑friction surfaces preferred  

---

## ALU Materials

### Mechanical Summing Element
- Brass gears  
- Steel shafts  
- Hardwood or aluminum frame  

### Bearings and Bushings
- Bronze bushings  
- Nylon or Delrin acceptable  
- Avoid ball bearings (unnecessary precision)  

### Linkages
- Steel rods  
- Brass pivots  
- Wooden levers for large‑scale builds  

---

## Output Unit Materials

### Printer or Typewheel
- Aluminum or brass wheel  
- Steel type slugs  
- Paper feed rollers (rubber or silicone)  

### Actuation
- Relay‑driven solenoids  
- Lever‑based selectors  

### Paper
- Standard receipt or adding‑machine paper  
- Archival paper for civic records  

---

## Finishes and Protection

### Wood Finish
- Linseed oil  
- Shellac  
- Light varnish  

### Metal Protection
- Light machine oil  
- Paste wax  
- Anti‑corrosion spray for steel components  

### Electrical Protection
- Heat‑shrink tubing  
- Insulating varnish for coils  

---

## Salvage Sources

The machine is designed to be buildable from:

- old telephony equipment  
- surplus relays  
- typewriters  
- adding machines  
- scrap wood and metal  
- laboratory connectors  
- automotive wiring  

Salvage is encouraged where safe and appropriate.

---

## Summary

The materials specified here ensure the relay computer remains:

- buildable with common tools  
- maintainable over decades  
- resilient to drift and wear  
- reproducible across workshops  

These materials form the physical foundation of the machine’s architecture.
