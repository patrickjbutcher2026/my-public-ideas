# Safety Protocols

This appendix defines the safety procedures required for operating, maintaining, and repairing the relay computer. Although the machine is low‑voltage and mechanically simple, it contains moving parts, electrical components, and pinch points that require responsible handling.

Safety is part of the machine’s civic design: predictable, teachable, and easy to enforce.

---

## Core Safety Principles

- Protect the operator  
- Protect the machine  
- Prevent silent failure  
- Ensure all actions are reversible  
- Maintain clear lines of responsibility  

These principles apply to every interaction with the machine.

---

## Electrical Safety

### Voltage and Current
- The machine operates at 12 V DC  
- Even low voltage can cause burns or shorts under fault conditions  

### Required Practices
- Always disconnect power before servicing  
- Use a fused power source (3–5 A)  
- Inspect wiring for fraying or exposed conductors  
- Avoid metal tools near energized relay banks  

### Prohibited Actions
- Bypassing fuses  
- Running the machine from unregulated or noisy supplies  
- Touching relay contacts while powered  

---

## Mechanical Safety

### Moving Parts
- Tape reader sprocket  
- ALU gears and linkages  
- Output unit mechanisms  

### Required Practices
- Keep hands clear of moving assemblies  
- Tie back loose clothing and hair  
- Do not operate with covers removed unless in diagnostic mode  
- Use the hand‑crank or pedal generator with stable footing  

### Pinch Points
- Sprocket wheel  
- Gear trains  
- Lever linkages  

Operators must remain aware of these areas during operation.

---

## Thermal Safety

### Heat Sources
- Relay coils during extended operation  
- Power distribution rails  
- Output unit solenoids  

### Required Practices
- Allow cooling periods during long jobs  
- Ensure ventilation around relay banks  
- Do not block airflow with papers or tools  

---

## Chemical and Cleaning Safety

### Approved Cleaning Materials
- Isopropyl alcohol (90%+)  
- Lint‑free cards  
- Light machine oil  
- Paste wax for metal protection  

### Prohibited Materials
- Abrasive cleaners  
- Water‑based solvents  
- Aerosol lubricants near contacts  

### Handling Notes
- Use alcohol sparingly  
- Avoid inhaling fumes  
- Store chemicals away from heat sources  

---

## Tape Handling Safety

### Risks
- Paper cuts  
- Tape jams  
- Misfeeds causing mechanical strain  

### Required Practices
- Feed tape gently into the reader  
- Keep tape edges smooth and free of tears  
- Stop the machine immediately if tape misalignment occurs  

---

## Plugboard Safety

### Electrical and Mechanical Risks
- Bent pins  
- Loose patch cords  
- Accidental shorting  

### Required Practices
- Install or remove cords only when powered off  
- Tug‑test each connection  
- Replace damaged cords immediately  

---

## Human‑Powered Generator Safety

### Hand‑Crank
- Maintain a firm grip  
- Do not exceed comfortable speed  
- Keep bystanders clear of crank radius  

### Pedal Generator
- Ensure stable seating  
- Keep clothing away from chain or belt  
- Do not pedal at unsafe speeds  

---

## Emergency Procedures

### If the Machine Behaves Abnormally
- Stop power immediately  
- Remove tape  
- Document the anomaly in the logbook  
- Inspect relay banks and wiring before restarting  

### If a Component Fails
- Power down  
- Tag the machine as out of service  
- Replace or repair the component  
- Log the event and corrective action  

### If an Operator Is Injured
- Stop the machine  
- Provide first aid  
- Record the incident  
- Review safety compliance  

---

## Training Requirements

Operators must be trained in:

- basic electrical safety  
- mechanical awareness  
- maintenance procedures  
- emergency shutdown  
- logbook documentation  

Training is part of the machine’s civic stewardship.

---

## Summary

These safety protocols ensure the relay computer remains:

- safe to operate  
- safe to maintain  
- predictable under fault conditions  
- suitable for community and civic environments  

Safety is not an add‑on — it is part of the architecture.
