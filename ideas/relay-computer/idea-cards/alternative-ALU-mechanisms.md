# Idea Card: Alternative ALU Mechanisms

This idea card explores alternative designs for the Arithmetic Logic Unit (ALU) beyond the baseline mechanical summing mechanism. These concepts are speculative and optional — they are not part of the core architecture but may inspire future upgrades or experimental builds.

The goal is to expand the design space while preserving transparency, maintainability, and workshop‑level feasibility.

---

## Concept 1: Lever-Based Differential Adder

A purely mechanical adder using weighted levers and pivot points.

### Characteristics
- Extremely visible operation  
- Low inertia  
- Easy to fabricate from wood or metal  
- Good for educational builds  

### Limitations
- Limited precision  
- Sensitive to friction and wear  

### Notes
This mechanism resembles early analog computing linkages.

---

## Concept 2: Gear Train Summing Unit

A compact gear‑based adder using small brass or steel gears.

### Characteristics
- Smooth operation  
- High repeatability  
- Compatible with relay‑driven selectors  
- Good for multi-bit addition  

### Limitations
- Requires careful alignment  
- More difficult to inspect visually  

### Notes
This is the baseline ALU mechanism in the main architecture.

---

## Concept 3: Ratchet-and-Pawl Incrementer

A stepwise mechanical incrementer driven by relay pulses.

### Characteristics
- Simple construction  
- Audible feedback  
- Good for counters and timers  

### Limitations
- Only supports increment/decrement  
- Not suitable for general arithmetic  

### Notes
Useful as a subsystem rather than a full ALU.

---

## Concept 4: Relay-Only Binary Adder

A fully electrical ALU using relay logic instead of mechanical summing.

### Characteristics
- No moving mechanical parts  
- Faster switching  
- Easier to expand to more bits  

### Limitations
- Higher relay count  
- Less visually intuitive  
- Increased power consumption  

### Notes
This design trades transparency for speed and scalability.

---

## Concept 5: Weighted Pulley Summing System

A hybrid analog‑digital mechanism using small weights and pulleys.

### Characteristics
- Extremely visible  
- Smooth analog summation  
- Easy to prototype  

### Limitations
- Sensitive to vibration  
- Requires calibration  
- Not ideal for portable builds  

### Notes
Inspired by early scientific instruments.

---

## Concept 6: Magnetic Coupling Adder

A gearless mechanism using magnetic coupling between rotating elements.

### Characteristics
- Low friction  
- Quiet operation  
- Resistant to dust  

### Limitations
- Requires consistent magnet strength  
- Harder to fabricate with hand tools  

### Notes
A modern twist on mechanical computation.

---

## Summary

These alternative ALU mechanisms provide a range of options for:

- educational builds  
- experimental prototypes  
- future upgrades  
- community variations  

None of these designs replace the baseline ALU, but each expands the machine’s design space and invites further exploration.
